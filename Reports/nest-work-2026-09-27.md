# Is the colony building a nest? The scoreboard and the nest funnel

*Measurement, 2026-09-27. `engine` / `lab`. Instruments and switches only:
nothing in any game changed. Lane note:
[`lanes/nest-mouth.md`](lanes/nest-mouth.md). Instruments:
[`instruments.md`](instruments.md)'s `digbox` row and
`scripts/nestscore.py`.*

## 0. The answer

**No — not yet.** The two new instruments agree. Both were run in `digbox`:
40 ants, no food, energy 1,000, 12 seeds, stops at 3,000, 6,000 and 12,000
frames.

- **By shape, the colony digs less like a nest than random digging does.**
  The nulls are 40 random walkers that start at the door and carve the same
  number of cells. The colony's excavation is less nest-like than theirs on
  12 of 12 seeds (panel percentile 0.00). It has **31 openings to the
  surface** where the owner's spec has one, and no chamber on any seed. It
  beats cells scattered uniformly or by its own row profile. So the colony is
  *digging near home*, which is not the same as *building*.
- **By work, 3% of cuts build anything** (2% on the worst seed). A cut
  counts as building when it opens new ground under a roof, its pellet is
  carried out, and the cell is still open 1,500 frames later.
  - The failure is the rate, not the reach: 97% of ants did every step of
    that at least once.
- **Where the digging goes: 78% of it is churn.**
  - 36% of cuts dig the spoil heaps above the old surface.
  - 42% re-cut a cell already dug or filled.
  - Of those re-cuts, 59% are tunnel lining, 36% soil and 5% spoil.
    Only 10% of pellets land below the surface, so most of the re-cut
    ground is the burrow falling back into itself. The spoil being put back
    is the smaller part.
- **The spoil does leave the ground, but most of it does not stay out.**
  90% of pellets land above the old surface, and 74% are set down beside
  the digger, at the lip of the hole they came out of. **Corrected the same
  day** ([`nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md`](nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md)):
  64% of pellets are put down where they cannot stay, on an ant's back or
  over a hole. They turn to loose soil within ten frames and fall, so the
  share that stays out as spoil is nearer a third.

## 1. The instruments

**The scoreboard** (in `digbox`, on by default; `nulls=0` turns it off)
scores the dug room as a mask at every stop.

- **Seven shape metrics,** each with a fixed "better" direction: mouths
  (distance from one), reach to the outside, largest piece, roofed share,
  depth, middle-half width, bore.
- **Four null models,** each carving the same number of cells with no nest
  logic:
  - uniform;
  - the colony's own row profile;
  - a blob grown from the door (eden);
  - random-walk diggers from the door (walkers, persistence 0.8, one per
    ant).
- **Output:** the colony's percentile among 200 draws of each null, plus a
  panel median. 0.5 means "like random digging".
- **Selftest:** a hand-carved ideal nest must score high, and each null
  scored against itself must land in the middle.

**The nest funnel** (on by default; `nofunnel` turns it off) is the per-ant
counterpart of the foraging funnel.

- **Stages.** Every ant is booked at the furthest stage it ever reached:
  1. faced diggable ground with its jaws free;
  2. cut a cell;
  3. cut new ground;
  4. cut new ground under a roof;
  5. carried that pellet out above the old surface;
  6. the cut still open 1,500 frames later.
- **Ledgers.** Two `LEDGER` lines put every cut and every pellet in exactly
  one bucket, reconciled against the engine's `digs` and `spoil_dumped`.
  - The remainder the funnel cannot place is printed on every run: 337 of
    95,092 digs (0.35%) and 1,001 of 94,673 pellets (1.06%) over 48 runs.
  - The first version placed only 64% of digs. That is because a digger acts
    and then steps into the cell it has just cut, so the cell reads as an
    animal rather than as empty. The ledger is what showed it.
- **It cannot change the run.** It reads the world between frames. All 48
  runs of the scored batch reproduce their census rows byte for byte with it
  on.
- **Positive control** (`digbox selftest`). One ant is driven by hand
  through four cases:
  - a complete cycle, which must book exactly one;
  - a pellet put back in its own hole: a refill, not a cycle;
  - a cut that caves in before 1,500 frames: carried out, not built;
  - a dig that changed no cell, which must land in `target mismatch`.

  It was watched go red: with the lasting check forced open, the caved-in
  cut counted as built.

**The high-water mark saturates, and that is itself the finding.** Over
12,000 frames nearly every ant reaches every stage once by chance. The stages
cannot tell building from scratching; the share of cuts that built can.

- **Scouting is not what drives it.** With it off, the funnel is the default

- **`SPOIL_HAUL` had never been measured.** It re-aims a spoil-laden ant at
  the door, and at 40 ants it changes nothing.

  carrier's next dig.

  its arms are shown to differ from default on every seed.
  - It takes pellets off the heaps (pellets below 10% → 4%; heap cuts
    36% → 28%).
  - It builds nothing more.

## 4. What the churn is made of

The biggest bucket is a cell already dug or filled, cut again: 42% of all
cuts. The ledger now names what each such cell was at the moment it was cut
again (median / worst seed):

| arm | lining | soil | spoil |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| default | 59% / 61% | 36% / 42% | 5% / 7% |
| `DIG_DOWN=1.0` | 63% / 65% | 29% / 30% | 8% / 13% |

**Holes refill mainly by falling in, not by being filled.** Only 10% of
pellets land below the old surface, and 5% of re-cuts are spoil. The rest is
ground that fell back into a dug cell. Most of that was then packed into
lining by a neighbour's `line_burrow` before somebody cut it again.

**Where the fill comes from.** Measured the same day with a refill ledger
added to the funnel. **Its first reading was wrong, and is corrected
here.**

It said the burrow is refilled by its own bank, soil falling in half from
the roof and half from the side, and not by the spoil heaps, whose share
was 0%. Two things in `update_powder` undo that reading, found by reading
it afterwards:
- **Worked ground never falls as itself.** Lining and pellets are
  `self_supporting`. A pellet whose footing is dug away, or lining with air
  below and fewer than three neighbours, turns into `soil` where it stands,
  and that soil falls on a later visit. So what arrives in a dug cell is
  `soil` whatever it was, and the 0% spoil was guaranteed by construction.
- **The ledger booked passes, not fills.** A grain falling down a dug shaft
  crosses one cell a frame and booked a refill at each, "from the cell
  above" each time. So the half-and-half split was the grains' path, and
  the 3,281 "refills" a run were mostly grains passing through.

The corrected ledger counts two things instead. **A dug cell still ground
100 frames after it filled.** And **worked ground turned loose where it
stands**, which is the supply the falling grains come from (12 seeds,
frame 12,000, median; worst seed for the shares):

| arm | grains passing through dug cells | dug cells refilled and still full | worked ground turned loose | of it: pellets in the heaps above the old surface | lining above | pellets below | lining below |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default | 3,094 | 1,148 by falls, 60 by pellets | 1,563 | 78% (81%) | 13% | 6% | 2% |
| `DIG_DOWN=1.0` | 4,406 | 1,490 by falls, 180 by pellets | 1,882 | 65% | 10% | 19% | 5% |

**The largest identified supply of the fill is the spoil heaps.** About two
thirds of every pellet set down turns loose in place. This section first
said *mostly when ants dig out from under the heap*. That was a guess, and
**traced per pellet it is wrong**
([`nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md`](nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md) §1):
95% of the pellets that turn loose above the surface had stood ten frames or
less. They were put down on the carrier's own back (46%), on another ant
(24%) or over air (25%), which the drop site allows and the footing rule
does not. Undercutting is real but smaller, and a cut packs the pellets
beside it into lining, so it shows as lining crumbling. The bank's
own share is not measured: a grain carries no identity, and bank soil
exposed where worked ground turned loose falls as the same `soil`. About
1,150 dug cells a run fill and stay filled, against 851 re-cuts, so most
of the re-cutting is digging those fills back out.

**The cut itself is mostly into lining, and that is by construction.**
`line_burrow` packs all eight neighbours of every cut, so the face ahead of
a tunnel is always lining. Of cuts into new ground, 87% were lining on seed
1. That is not waste: it is how a tunnel advances. The waste is the re-cut
column.

## 5. Spoil: the goal, and the distance to it

**The goal**, as the record states it:

- **The biology.** The cycle is grab at the face, climb, exit, carry out,
  drop (Aguilar 2018). About 2.5% of excavated soil stays below ground
  (Tschinkel 2015). Outside, each exiting ant walks a few body lengths and
  drops, which builds the crater. See `nest-excavation-mechanics-2026-09-19.md`
  §3 and §11.
- **The owner's rulings.**
  - *Where a pellet lands is for the ants to solve, through the brain, and
    not a placement rule in `act`* (`dead-ends.md`, the two hand-written
    rules, 2026-08-31).
  - *Spoil must not hang in the sky*, reported three times. That is why,
    since §Z18, a pellet needs a footing (`needs_footing`): with nothing
    ground under it, it turns into loose soil where it stands.

**How close we are, at 40 ants:**

- **Out of the ground: not as close as it looks.** 90% of pellets end
  above the old surface, against ~97.5% for a real colony. At 300 ants the
  same engine measured 19% (2026-09-20), so colony size decides where the
  digging happens. But two thirds of all pellets are set on an ant or over a
  hole and fall back as loose soil within ten frames
  ([`nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md`](nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md)),
  so what stays out as spoil is nearer a third.
- **Away from the hole: not at all.** 74% of pellets are set beside the
  digger, at the lip. The heap then becomes 36% of all digging.

**Why the lip.** `DropSpoil` is authored as a copy of the old food drop:
`AtNest +0.9, Carrying +0.2`, and `ant.ron`'s comment says so ("the spoil
dump, unchanged"). At home the drop is therefore certain. The digging is
concentrated at home too (`Dig` ≈ 0.8 at the nest against ≈ 0.13–0.15
away). So a pellet dug at home is put down at home.

Flipping the home term moves the pellets but not the outcome (§3). **The
spoil is not the main loss. The burrow refilling itself is (§4).**

## 6. The door + dug-mouth default, re-measured on current `main`

The switches are `NEST_DOOR=2 NEST_SHAFT=6 NEST_HOME=mouth`, now with
scouting on:

- **Colony bed, 24 seeds:** starved 201 → 105 (p = 0.001). It still halves
  starvation where the food is out in the open.
- **Lab, 12 seeds, 120,000 frames** (seeds where the arm is lower):

| | default | door + mouth | + `UNPACK` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| births | 530 | 362 (lower on 8, p 0.39) | 468 (lower on 9) |
| food eaten | 1.15 M | 0.85 M (lower on 10, **p 0.04**) | 0.99 M (lower on 8) |
| colony-frames | 9.57 M | 6.21 M (lower on 9, p 0.15) | 7.77 M (lower on 9) |
| colonies lost | 0 | 1 | 3 |

**It stays off.** The owner prefers options on by default "unless there is
a good reason not to". A lab colony that eats less on 10 of 12 seeds and
loses one colony in twelve is that reason.

What would change it is the mechanism that `nest-mouth-2026-09-26.md`
already traced: the lab's dug mouth is buried by frame 30,600 on 11–12 of
12 seeds, under the colony's own delivered food and the roots that grow in
it. A colony that kept its mouth clear would reopen the question. That is
nest work, and this lane's.

## 7. Next steps, each scored on these two instruments

1. **Stop the heaps feeding the holes.** The corrected refill ledger (§4)
   puts 78% of worked ground turning loose in the pellets heaped above the
   old surface. **Done the same day, and the cause is not the one this step
   named**
   ([`nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md`](nest-spoil-footing-2026-09-27.md)).
   It said "those pellets turn loose when ants dig out from under them
   (36% of all cuts are in the heaps)". Traced, 64% of pellets are put down
   with no footing and turn loose within ten frames.
   `PIXEL_PHYSICS_SPOIL_FOOTING=ground` and `PIXEL_PHYSICS_SPOIL_PACKS=off`
   are that report's switches. The loose soil does fall into the pits and
   mouths nearby. The two ways this step proposed to break the loop:
   - pellets set down away from the holes they came from, by the genome
     (the drop-away variant took heap cuts 36% -> 28% but built no more,
     §3);
   - diggers that leave their own heaps alone, which is the dig marker in
     step 3 from the other side.

   Whichever is tried, the ledger's standing-fill count is what it has to
   move. The walls themselves are the smaller supply: lining is 15% of what
   turns loose. A sub-agent's map of the fall rules, the lining and the
   prior attempts is the starting point:
   - worked ground converts in place and never falls as itself;
   - `attached` is not read for powders, so `digbox`'s attached soil does
     not hold;
   - the lining stays one cell thick.
2. **`DIG_DOWN` stays off: measured as a default the same day.** It doubles
   the building share in `digbox`, a box with no food. On the colony bed it
   more than doubles starvation: 201 -> 460 of 480, with the share of ants
   that ever reached the food falling from 67% to 17%. Ants at home, where
   `Dig` is about 0.8, turn down and dig instead of going out. The lab ties
   on every column. `dead-ends.md`'s `DIG_DOWN` entry carries the numbers.
3. **A dig marker at the face** (Toffin et al. 2009): dig where digging
   just happened, with a Hill term, "which is why one shaft wins over many
   shallow pits". This is the direct attack on 31 mouths.
   - Only the density half of Toffin has been tried (`(Crowding, Dig)`,
     withdrawn 2026-09-02). The marker half has no entry in `dead-ends.md`.
   - It needs a per-cell channel, so it has to name its writer and its
     reader before building.
4. **`DropSpoil` as a spoil drop rather than a food drop:** revisit only
   together with 1–3. Alone it moves pellets and builds nothing.
