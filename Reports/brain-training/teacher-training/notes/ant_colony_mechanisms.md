## 1. Response-threshold models: what makes specialists vs generalists?

### Takeaway
A fixed response-threshold model (workers act when a task's locally sensed stimulus exceeds their own threshold, and acting lowers the stimulus) needs *inter-individual threshold variation* to produce specialists; with identical workers, specialisation can still emerge if thresholds are *self-reinforcing* (doing a task lowers your threshold for it; not doing it raises it). But thresholds alone do not reproduce real data well: tracked colonies of a clonal ant need extra ingredients — variation in task *efficiency* and in *demand* (larval hunger) — and threshold mechanisms struggle to hit biased work ratios.

### Cited findings
- Theraulaz, Bonabeau & Deneubourg (1998) proposed variable response thresholds: a worker's threshold drops when it performs a task and rises when it does not; "this combined reinforcement process leads to the emergence of specialized workers" from initially identical workers. The model also predicts hysteresis: if specialists are removed and later reintroduced, the colony does not return to its prior state, and the gap widens with longer separation. — [Theraulaz et al. 1998, Proc R Soc B (PMC1688885)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1688885); [SFI working paper](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wop/safiwp/98-01-006.html)
- Ulrich, Kawakatsu, Tokita, Saragosti, Chandra, Tarnita & Kronauer (2021) tracked 120 colonies of the clonal raider ant (*Ooceraea biroi*) with controlled genetic, morphological and demographic (age) composition. Each source of variation produced a *distinct* pattern, "ranging from the amplification to the dampening of inherent behavioral differences in heterogeneous colonies"; larvae modulate interactions between adults. "Models based on threshold variation alone only partially recapitulate these empirical patterns"; adding variability in **task efficiency among adults** and **task demand among larvae** accounted for all observed phenomena. — According to PubMed: [Ulrich et al. 2021, PLoS Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3001269](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001269)
- Duarte, Pen, Keller & Weissing (2012): with evolvable thresholds, a 3:1 biased distribution of workers over tasks "is not easily achievable by a threshold mechanism, even under strong selection." — According to PubMed: [Duarte et al. 2012, Behav Ecol Sociobiol, doi:10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2)
- Lichocki, Tarapore, Keller & Floreano (2012) show the *deterministic* threshold model constrains worker behavioural flexibility under some stimulus conditions, and the *probabilistic* threshold model fails to produce a precise colony response to varying stimuli; adding variables that weigh stimuli fixes this, and threshold models can be written as artificial neural networks. Published in *American Naturalist* 179(3):391–400 (not PLoS ONE as the brief stated). — [Lichocki et al. 2012 (EPFL record)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/182822/files/lichocki12b.pdf?version=1); [Google Research listing](https://research.google/pubs/neural-networks-as-mechanisms-to-regulate-division-of-labor/)
- Duarte, Weissing, Pen & Keller (2011, *Annu Rev Ecol Evol Syst* 42:91–110) review factors known to affect task choice and the main self-organisation models, arguing that self-organisation models give a proximate account only and need an evolutionary perspective. — [Duarte et al. 2011 (record)](https://isidore.science/document/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-102710-145017); [PDF host](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/publications/_pdf/du_eaAREES11.pdf)
- Gordon (2016) argues "division of labor" is misleading because "there is little evidence for persistent individual specialization in task"; task allocation is a distributed process whose advantage is resilience, and focus should be on how individuals interact with each other and their environment. — According to PubMed: [Gordon 2016, Behav Ecol Sociobiol, doi:10.1007/s00265-015-2045-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-015-2045-3)
- Experience-based reinforcement exists in real ants: Ravary et al. (2007, *Current Biology*) experimentally made some workers succeed at every foraging attempt and others always fail; successful ones kept a high propensity to forage, failing ones reduced foraging, and a month later the successful ones were still foraging while the failures had moved into brood care — "individual experience alone can generate lasting division of labor." (Species named in the literature as *Cerapachys biroi* = *Ooceraea biroi*; species not confirmed in the retrieved abstract.) — [AntKey summary of Ravary et al. 2007](https://antkey.myspecies.info/en/content/individual-experience-alone-can-generate-lasting-division-labor-ants)
- "Specialization does not predict individual efficiency in an ant" (Dornhaus 2008, PLoS Biol) — title found in search; abstract not retrieved. — [PMC2586388](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2586388/)

### Inferences
- Ingredients for specialists, in increasing order of what they demand from a shared-genome brain: (a) a locally sensed **task stimulus** that the task itself reduces (negative feedback through the environment); (b) **some individual difference** in responsiveness — either fixed (genotype, size, age) or **acquired through a self-reinforcing internal state** (a memory unit that integrates "I did task X / succeeded at X"). With one shared genome and no morphological variation, (b) must come from age, energy, position, or a learned/memory state — the Theraulaz 1998 reinforcement rule is precisely a one-variable-per-task memory with up/down drift.
- For a neural brain, the Theraulaz rule translates to: memory unit m_X += α when performing X, −β otherwise, and the action logit for X increases with m_X. A teacher rule could be written as "do X if stimulus_X × (1 + m_X) > θ".
- Ravary 2007 suggests **success** (not mere performance) is a sufficient reinforcing signal — a memory input of recent foraging success is biologically motivated.
- Ulrich 2021 implies that **brood demand** (hunger signal from larvae) and **efficiency differences** matter; a sim without brood demand signals may produce flatter DOL than real ants.

### Gaps
- Bonabeau, Theraulaz & Deneubourg 1996 (*Proc R Soc B* 263:1565) fixed-threshold formula not retrieved this session. **[background, unverified this session]**: response probability T_θ(s) = s^n / (s^n + θ^n) with n = 2; stimulus s grows at a constant rate δ and decreases in proportion to the number of workers performing the task; model was fitted to Wilson's (1984) *Pheidole* minor-removal experiments, where majors take up minor tasks when the minor:major ratio is lowered.
- Beshers & Fewell 2001 (*Annu Rev Entomol* 46:413) review not retrieved. **[background]**: it classifies models into response thresholds, integrated threshold–information transfer, self-reinforcement, foraging-for-work, social inhibition, and network task allocation.
- No quantitative values for reinforcement rates (ξ, φ in Theraulaz 1998) were retrieved.

## 2. Foraging-for-work, age polyethism, spatial fidelity, interaction-rate regulation, task switching

### Takeaway
In real ants, the strongest individually sensable correlates of task are **age** and **where in the nest the worker currently is** (spatial fidelity); interaction structure follows location. Short-term allocation (how many forage now) is regulated by **encounter rate** with returning successful foragers — a local, Poisson-like rate signal — not by global information.

### Cited findings
- Mersch, Crespi & Keller (2013, *Science* 340:1090): automated tracking of individually tagged workers in six *Camponotus fellah* colonies over 41 days; network analysis of >9 million interactions revealed **three groups** with distinct behavioural repertoires; workers **move between groups with age**; interactions were far more frequent within than between groups; "age-related changes in spatial location appear to primarily structure within-colony interactions" — spatial fidelity is a key regulator. — [Mersch et al. 2013 (EPFL record)](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/92751)
- Harvester ant (*Pogonomyrmex barbatus*) foraging: colonies regulate the rate at which outgoing foragers leave using the rate of brief antennal contacts inside the nest between foragers *returning with food* and outgoing foragers; the model needs no spatial information (no trails, seeds retrieved singly); return intervals are well described as a Poisson process; simulated outgoing rates correlated with observed returning rates similarly to the data. — [Prabhakar, Dektar & Gordon 2012, PLoS Comput Biol (PMC3426560)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3426560/)
- A later analysis models harvester foraging as a closed-loop **excitable system**; feedback from outgoing foragers that return stabilises incoming and outgoing rates to a common value. — [Pagliara et al. 2018 (PMC6294393)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6294393/)
- Gordon (2002) "The regulation of foraging activity in red harvester ant colonies" (*Am Nat*). — [link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/339461) (abstract not retrieved)
- Pinter-Wollman et al. 2013 (*Animal Behaviour*) on harvester ant interaction networks — [PDF](https://pinterwollmanlab.eeb.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/86/2016/05/Pinter-Wollman-et-al-2013-AB.pdf) (not read)
- Duarte, Scholtens & Weissing (2012) evolved neural networks for task choice: with a simple feedforward network, **only genetically determined specialisation** was possible; adding **self-feedback** (memory of previous experience) increased flexibility and worker specialisation evolved under a wider parameter range. — According to PubMed: [Duarte et al. 2012, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002430](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002430)
- Experience (success/failure) reshapes task choice and persists for a month (Ravary et al. 2007, above). — [AntKey](https://antkey.myspecies.info/en/content/individual-experience-alone-can-generate-lasting-division-labor-ants)
- Bipartite ant–task network analysis reported task groups and absence of colony-level daily activity rhythm (bioRxiv 2020). — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.03.28.013128.full.pdf) (not read in detail)

### Inferences
- Senses supported for the brain: **age** (drives position, hence task), **at-nest / depth / distance-from-brood** (spatial fidelity), **recent encounter rate** with laden nestmates (Gordon's mechanism — a leaky-integrator of "met a returning forager carrying food"), **carrying**, and a **memory of success**. Crowding is a reasonable proxy for encounter rate only if it distinguishes *who* is encountered (laden vs not).
- Foraging-for-work (Tofts & Franks) maps naturally onto "age → drift outward + do whatever task is locally present": a spatial model where task follows location and location follows age plus local work availability. This needs no explicit task-identity sense — it needs position-related senses plus the local stimulus.
- A teacher rule for exit decisions: "leave nest if (count of laden returners met in last K ticks) > threshold", with threshold possibly modulated by energy.

### Gaps
- Tofts & Franks 1992 (*TREE* "Doing the right thing: ants, honeybees and naked mole-rats") and Franks & Tofts 1994 foraging-for-work model: not retrieved. **[background]**: workers are born in the centre (brood area) and move outward to find work; age polyethism emerges as a by-product of spatial work availability; criticised for predicting strong correlation between task and location and for weaker fit to rapid task switching.
- Seeley 1982 (honeybee age polyethism; *Behav Ecol Sociobiol*) and Robinson 1992 (*Annu Rev Entomol*) not retrieved. **[background]**: honeybee workers progress cleaning → brood care → food handling → foraging at ~3 weeks of age; juvenile hormone rises with age and accelerates foraging onset; social inhibition (Huang & Robinson 1992) — contact with older foragers delays foraging onset, so the colony's age structure self-adjusts.
- Gordon & Mehdiabadi 1999 / Greene & Gordon 2003 (*Nature*, cuticular hydrocarbons of patrollers stimulate foraging) — **[background]** only.
- No numbers retrieved on proportions of workers inactive. **[background]**: in *Temnothorax*, roughly 40–50% of workers are inactive at any time and ~25% are "always inactive" (Charbonneau & Dornhaus 2015) — verify.

## 3. Nest excavation: which local signals set room size and stop digging?

### Takeaway
Excavated volume tracks worker number with no global measurement of size: digging follows a **logistic** curve (positive feedback via recruitment/digging-site pheromone, then saturation), and volume at saturation is roughly **proportional to workers**. Candidate local stop/size signals: **crowding/density at the dig face** (more space → fewer encounters → less stimulation), **decaying pheromone in the material** (pheromone lifetime controls structure growth), **body-size templates** (roof height), and **brood-cluster templates** (wall distance). Efficient excavation in narrow tunnels additionally requires **idleness** and **retreat on collision** — unequal workload is functional.

### Cited findings
- Buhl, Deneubourg, Grimal & Theraulaz (2005, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 58:9–17; online ~2004): groups of different sizes of *Messor sancta* given a thin uniform sand disk excavated gallery networks in <3 days; digging curve is **logistic** (positive feedback then negative feedback to saturation); **total excavated volume was almost proportional to the number of workers**; a model with pheromone-mediated recruitment as positive feedback reproduced logistic dynamics and the linear volume–population relation, and predicted that digging nearly stops **even without an explicit negative-feedback term**. — [search summary of Buhl et al.](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00093178v1); [ULB record](https://difusion.ulb.ac.be/vufind/Record/ULB-DIPOT:oai:dipot.ulb.ac.be:2013/96612/Details)
- Gautrais, Buhl et al. (2014, PLoS ONE) on the same data: more workers → higher meshedness; transition from tree-like to highly meshed gallery networks with group size. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0109436.html); [PMC4198125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4198125)
- Rasse & Deneubourg (2001, *Evol Ecol Res*), secondhand: *Lasius niger* ants **gradually reduce their digging rate** as they excavate, and this "allows nest volume to be adapted to group size"; "workers adjust the size of their nests according to the population size of the colony." — [secondhand citation via search; Springer Insectes Sociaux](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00040-003-0719-8) (primary not retrieved)
- Toffin, Di Paolo, Campo, Detrain & Deneubourg (2009, *PNAS* 106:18616): in 2D digging experiments, a primary circular cavity transitions into a **ramified (branching) structure**; the transition occurs regardless of colony size but more often with more workers; a stochastic model indicates **density effects** are central to the shape transition. A *Lasius niger* comparison of 50 vs 300 workers fitted excavation with a saturating curve (cooperation parameter ≈1.72 for 300 ants vs 1.38 for 50 — from search snippet, unverified against full text). — [PMC2773997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773997)
- Toffin, Kindekens & Deneubourg (2010, *Proc R Soc B*): the two morphological transitions occur more frequently in a **cohesive** substrate than a granular one. — [PMC2982035](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2982035)
- Khuong et al. (2016, *PNAS* 113:1303): *Lasius niger* construction uses two interactions: (1) **stigmergic** amplification of deposits at locations marked by a **pheromone added to the building material**; (2) a **template**: ants use **their body size** as the cue for the height at which they start building a roof from pillars. A 3D stochastic model reproduces regularly spaced pillars and merging caps; **pheromone lifetime is a highly influential parameter controlling growth and form**. A 2024 review notes experimental evidence for a "cement pheromone" in *termites* is weak. — [Pherobase record](https://pherobase.com/literature/data/details/B/42788); [HAL](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02324165); [PMC4747701](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4747701); [2024 review PMC11006414](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11006414/)
- Franks & Deneubourg (1997, *Anim Behav* 54:779–796): *Temnothorax albipennis* (formerly *Leptothorax*) workers build a nest wall with **no direct communication and no explicit assessment of nest size**; stigmergic response to existing material. Invernizzi & Ruxton (2021) replicated the agent-based model: limited building material degrades the final structure, and nest size adjusting to colony size over time seems to conflict with the 1997 analytical model's predictions. — [Invernizzi & Ruxton 2021, Anim Behav Cogn](https://www.animalbehaviorandcognition.org/article.php?id=1264); [PDF](https://www.animalbehaviorandcognition.org/uploads/journals/33/AB_C_2021_Vol8(2)_Invernizzi_Ruxton.pdf)
- Aguilar et al. (2018, *Science* 361:672–677): fire ants (*Solenopsis invicta*), robots and models; in narrow tunnels digging performance is robustly optimised by **individual idleness and retreating**: idleness reduces flow-stopping clogs; **selective retreating** (reversing when encountering a jam) shortens dissolution time for the rare clusters that do form; ants select a tunnel density that maximises flux without global control (APS abstract). Press account: removing the hardest-working ants led others to compensate with no productivity loss (secondary). — [GaTech PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/science-2018.pdf); [SM](https://goldmanlab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/aan3891_Aguilar_SM.pdf); [APS abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2018/s51/10); [GaTech news](https://qbios.gatech.edu/node/351)
- Tschinkel field work: in *Camponotus socius*, nest **volume and area were closely and positively related to the number of workers**; *Odontomachus brunneus* nests held 11–177 workers in 2–17 chambers with 28–340 cm² chamber floor. Mikheyev & Tschinkel 2004 (*Insectes Sociaux*) is about *Formica pallidefulva* (architecture, costs and rules of excavation), not fire ants as the brief implied. — [Tschinkel 2005, J Insect Sci (Camponotus socius)](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Insect-Science/volume-5/issue-9/031.005.0901/The-nest-architecture-of-the-ant-span-classgenus-speciesCamponotus-socius/10.1673/031.005.0901.pdf); [Mikheyev & Tschinkel 2004 PDF](https://www.bio.fsu.edu/~tschink/publications/2004-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **No ant measures nest volume.** Sufficient local signals for "dig more / stop": (a) **local crowding / encounter rate** — when workers per unit of open space is high, digging stimulation persists; as space grows, density at the face drops and digging decays (density-driven, Toffin; Rasse's declining digging rate is consistent with this); (b) a **dig-site marker that decays** (pheromone/recruitment in Buhl; pheromone in material in Khuong) — the decay constant sets size and form; (c) **template cues** — own body size for ceiling height; distance from brood cluster for wall placement.
- For the brain: crowding (at the dig face, not globally), depth, a local "fresh-dig-marker" scent, and at-nest/near-brood are the senses that matter. A teacher rule: "dig if at nest edge AND local crowding > c AND fresh-dig scent present; deposit spoil away from brood." Expected emergent target: volume ∝ N with saturating (logistic) time course.
- Aguilar implies a training arena with **narrow tunnels** rewards *idleness and backing off on collision*; a brain that always digs will clog. Unequal workload is a feature to expect, not a bug to train away.

### Gaps
- Exact Buhl et al. group sizes and volumes (cm² per worker), and Rasse & Deneubourg numbers — full texts not retrieved. **[background, unverified]**: Buhl et al. used groups of roughly 50–200 *Messor* workers; volume per worker roughly constant.
- Khuong pheromone half-life value and roof-height-to-body-length ratio not retrieved. **[background, unverified]**: building pheromone lifetime on the order of tens of minutes (~30 min); roofs initiated at ~body-length height (~ 3–4 mm in *L. niger*).
- Aguilar quantitative inequality not retrieved. **[background, unverified]**: roughly 30% of workers did ~70% of excavation (Lorenz-curve inequality), tunnel width ~ 1–2 body widths.
- Tschinkel fire ant scaling (nest volume vs colony size, chamber count) not retrieved. **[background]**: fire ant (*S. invicta*) nest volume increases roughly linearly with worker number/colony mass and nests grow by adding chambers/shafts (Tschinkel, 1999–2004 papers; Cassill, Tschinkel & Vinson 2002 on nest complexity vs group size [PDF](https://www.bio.fsu.edu/~tschink/publications/2002-1.pdf)).

## 4. Castes: morphological castes, caste-ratio regulation, and whether behavioural castes need morphology

### Takeaway
Morphological caste ratios are regulated by **negative feedback from existing adults**: in *Pheidole*, adult soldiers inhibit soldier development in larvae via contact (likely a contact pheromone), by raising the juvenile-hormone threshold larvae must exceed. Behavioural specialisation, however, does not require morphology: experience and age/location suffice in monomorphic species (e.g. clonal raider ants), and in polymorphic species the large caste flexibly takes up other tasks when the ratio is perturbed.

### Cited findings
- Wheeler & Nijhout (1984, *J Insect Physiol* 30:127–135): in *Pheidole bicarinata*, adult soldiers suppress soldier induction in methoprene-treated larvae; **suppression increases with increasing contact** between larvae and adult soldiers; a **contact soldier pheromone** is the most likely cause (authors' inference); suppression can be overridden by high methoprene doses during the JH-sensitive period; the **JH threshold for soldier development changes in the presence of soldiers**, giving the colony a way to regulate its caste ratio. — [Arizona Experts record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/soldier-determination-in-pheidole-bicarinata-inhibition-by-adult-/)
- Wheeler & Nijhout (1981, *Science*): topical juvenile hormone analog induces soldier development; (1983) soldier induction requires methoprene above a soldier-determining threshold during a critical period. — [Arizona Experts (JH role)](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/soldier-determination-in-ants-new-role-for-juvenile-hormone/); [methoprene effect](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/soldier-determination-in-pheidole-bicarinata-effect-of-methoprene/)
- Clonal raider ant: removing vs adding larvae drives transitions between reproduction and brood-care phases in adults (larvae inhibit reproduction and induce brood care) — brood presence is a strong behavioural switch in a species with no morphological worker castes. — According to PubMed: [Libbrecht, Oxley & Kronauer 2018, BMC Biol, doi:10.1186/s12915-018-0558-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-018-0558-8)
- Ulrich et al. 2021: morphological (size) variation, genetic variation and age variation each produce a different behavioural-organisation pattern; morphology is one source among several, not a prerequisite. — [doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3001269](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001269)
- Ravary et al. 2007: lasting specialisation from experience alone in a monomorphic species. — [AntKey](https://antkey.myspecies.info/en/content/individual-experience-alone-can-generate-lasting-division-labor-ants)

### Inferences
- Caste-ratio regulation is itself a **response-threshold mechanism at the developmental level**: P(larva → soldier) falls with the local density of soldier contacts. If the project ever adds castes, the simplest faithful rule is "larva becomes major with probability decreasing in recent contacts with majors (and increasing with nutrition)".
- With one shared genome and no morphology, behavioural castes require **state** that differs between individuals: age, energy, location, memory. These are exactly the senses listed in the project brief — sufficient in principle per Ravary 2007 and Mersch 2013.

### Gaps
- Oster & Wilson 1978 (*Caste and Ecology in the Social Insects*, Princeton UP) — not retrieved. **[background]**: ergonomic theory; colonies should evolve caste ratios that maximise net energy yield given task frequencies (linear-programming argument); number of physical castes predicted to match number of distinct task classes; physical castes are rare and mostly in large colonies.
- Wilson 1984 (*Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 16:89) *Pheidole* minor-removal experiment. **[background]**: when the minor:major ratio is lowered (roughly below 1:1), majors expand their repertoire to minor tasks — the experiment the Bonabeau 1996 threshold model was built to explain.
- No numbers retrieved on actual soldier proportions (*Pheidole* majors typically ~5–20% of workers **[background, unverified]**).

## 5. Evolutionary models: what makes division of labour evolve?

### Takeaway
Division of labour evolves (via branching of thresholds, or in evolved neural networks) mainly when **task switching is costly** and selection is on **total work done**; it is harder to evolve when selection is on matching an exact worker ratio. Neural network architecture matters: **feedforward-only** brains can only give genetically fixed specialisation, while **recurrent/self-feedback** brains evolve specialisation over a wider parameter range and achieve target work ratios more easily.

### Cited findings
- Duarte, Pen, Keller & Weissing (2012, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 66:947–957): with evolvable thresholds, DOL evolves through **evolutionary branching** of thresholds into workers differing in their tendency to take a task. "When selection is strongest on the amount of work performed, division of labor evolves **if switching tasks is costly**. When selection is strongest on worker distribution, division of labor is less likely to evolve." A biased distribution like 3:1 is not easily achieved; contrary to expectation, **multiple mating of foundresses impedes** the evolution of specialisation. — According to PubMed: [doi:10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2)
- Duarte, Scholtens & Weissing (2012, *PLoS Comput Biol* 8:e1002430): colony fitness = reduce idleness + achieve a predefined work ratio. Simple feedforward network → only genetically determined specialisation, and networks evolve a **biased work ratio to minimise idleness** even when an unbiased one is optimal. Adding **self-feedback** (previous experience) → specialisation evolves under a wider parameter range and optimal ratios are more easily achieved, "but still provide a challenge when combined with worker specialization." — According to PubMed: [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002430](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002430)
- Duarte thesis (2012), summarised: strong pressure to maximise work favours task **generalists**, while **task-switching costs favour specialisation**. — [search summary of Duarte thesis / review](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/publications/_pdf/thesis_Duarte_12.pdf)
- Lichocki et al. 2012 (*Am Nat*): threshold models are a special case of neural networks; adding stimulus-weighting variables gave efficient evolved allocation in a foraging scenario. — [EPFL](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/182822/files/lichocki12b.pdf?version=1)

### Inferences
- **Directly relevant to the project:** a shared-genome brain that should yield specialists needs (1) **recurrent memory** (the "memory units") so experience can differentiate individuals, and (2) an arena where **switching tasks costs something** (travel time between nest and food, a warm-up/inefficiency after switching, or spatial separation of tasks). Without switching costs, the evolutionary optimum is generalists, and the brain will not specialise no matter how good its senses are.
- Fitness that rewards **total work** plus a **switching cost** is the regime where DOL evolves; fitness that rewards an exact task ratio fights the mechanism.
- Expect evolved brains to accept some **idleness** and a biased work ratio; this is consistent with real ants (Aguilar; lazy-worker literature) and need not be penalised.

### Gaps
- Goldsby, Dornhaus, Kerr & Ofria 2012 (*PNAS* 109:13686, "Task-switching costs promote the evolution of division of labor and shifts in individuality") — not retrieved. **[background]**: Avida digital organisms evolved DOL when task-switching costs were present, and higher costs led to more DOL and more reproductive division.
- Tannenbaum 2007 (*J Theor Biol*, "When does division of labor lead to increased system output?") — not retrieved. **[background]**: DOL increases output when the cost of switching/transport between tasks is high relative to task time, and favours specialisation in larger groups.
- Colony-size effects (larger colonies → more specialisation; e.g. Jeanson, Fewell et al. 2007 *Am Nat*, Holbrook et al.) — not retrieved **[background]**.

## 6. Quantitative targets

### Takeaway
Usable targets: excavated volume ≈ linear in worker number with a logistic time course saturating within days; branching/meshedness increases with group size; foraging outflow tracks a Poisson return rate; three spatially-defined behavioural groups (nurse / intermediate / forager) traversed with age; switching costs required for specialists.

### Cited findings
- Volume ∝ workers, logistic dynamics, saturation in <3 days (Messor sancta, thin sand disk). — [Buhl et al.](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00093178v1)
- More workers → more meshed, less tree-like networks (Gautrais/Buhl 2014). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0109436.html)
- *Lasius niger*: 50 vs 300 worker groups; cooperation exponent ≈1.38 vs 1.72 in saturating excavation fits (snippet; verify). — [PMC2773997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773997)
- *Odontomachus brunneus*: 11–177 workers, 2–17 chambers, 28–340 cm² chamber floor; *Camponotus socius* nest volume and area closely, positively related to worker number. — [Tschinkel 2005](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Insect-Science/volume-5/issue-9/031.005.0901/The-nest-architecture-of-the-ant-span-classgenus-speciesCamponotus-socius/10.1673/031.005.0901.pdf)
- *Camponotus fellah*: 6 colonies, 41 days, >9 million interactions, 3 groups, age-driven transitions. — [Mersch et al. 2013](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/92751)
- Ravary 2007: experience-induced specialisation persisted ~1 month. — [AntKey](https://antkey.myspecies.info/en/content/individual-experience-alone-can-generate-lasting-division-labor-ants)
- Duarte 2012: 3:1 task ratios hard to hit with thresholds. — [doi:10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-012-1343-2)

### Inferences
- A practical arena check: run colonies of N and 2N with the same brain and confirm excavated area roughly doubles and digging rate decays logistically — this is the cleanest emergent "nest sized to colony" test, needing no global sense.
- A practical DOL check: measure per-ant task-repeat probability (P(same task next bout)) and its between-individual variance, with and without a switching cost in the arena.
