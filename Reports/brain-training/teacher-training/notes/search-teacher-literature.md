**Verification tags**

- **[abstract]**: the figure or claim appears in the paper's own abstract, as returned by SciSpace or quoted in a

- **[snippet]**: it comes from a secondary source, such as a search summary or a later paper describing the work.

## 6. What I looked for and did not find

- **A search teacher distilled into a network as small as ours (~700 weights).** None found, and the prior review reached
  the same conclusion: every distilled student it found has at least ~10,000 parameters. [not found]
- **A measured correlation between two actions' rollout outcomes from one cloned state, as a function of horizon.** Veness
  et al. and Yadav et al. are the nearest. Neither abstract gives the number, so ours has to be measured.
- **Per-target ablations in ExIt**, and **per-game network-versus-teacher gaps in Guo et al.**: these are in the full
  texts, which I could not read.
- **A search teacher in a stigmergic setting**, where agents coordinate only through marks such as scent: none found.

## 7. The three findings that matter most for the design

1. **Search used as one step of policy improvement, then copied into a classifier, is an old and well-tested recipe that
   works.** It is rollout classification policy iteration: Lagoudakis & Parr 2003, Dimitrakakis & Lagoudakis 2008, and
   Lazaric et al. 2016 for the theory. Tesauro & Galperin measured error rates falling by a factor of 5 or more from a
   single step. Expert iteration and AlphaZero repeat the step. **Two things decide the cost:**
   - which states are labelled and how many rollouts each gets: bandit allocation and early detection of a dominating
     action gave an order of magnitude;
   - the horizon: truncated rollouts with a value estimate beat full rollouts in backgammon.
2. **Paired rollouts work only if the random draws are matched by *event*, and the gain then depends on a correlation
   that has to be measured.** PEGASUS, Veness et al. and Yadav et al. support the method. Buffalo et al. 2026 make the
   point exactly: matching by draw index fails once the runs diverge, and keying draws on events is the fix, which is
   what `rng::stream` already does. Wright & Ramsay show common random numbers can *increase* variance. Comparing
   options on the mean over futures avoids the hindsight trap of Yoon et al.; picking the best per future falls into it.
3. **A planner or search teacher has two documented ways to fail.**
   - **It can be worse than its base policy** if its pruning or choice rule is wrong (Issakkimuthu et al. 2020).
   - **It can know more than the student can act on.** Coaching, PLATO's staying close to the learner, and Grill's KL to
     the prior all answer this by keeping the target near what the learner can do. Choudhury et al. show a clairvoyant
     oracle can still teach a student with partial information, if the student is trained on cost-to-go values in its
     own states.
