Why some mould goes grey, and three ways to draw it

What grey means

Mould is yellow when food reaches it and grey when it doesn't. Food reaches a bit of tube when there's a pile with food left less than 70 cells away along the tube. Three things leave tube outside that reach:

  • The pile ran out. The tube that was eating there has nothing left in reach. This is most of the grey you see, and nearly all of it on Running Dry, where every pile runs out on purpose.
  • A cut. A snip or the storm cuts off a piece. That piece now has only its own piles, and if it has none it starves.
  • Explorers. Thin feelers sent out past 70 cells from any food are grey until they find some.

Grey mould is still alive. It is eating its last share, and dies off over the next several seconds unless it reconnects to food.

Why it looks bad

Each cell was either yellow or grey, with nothing in between. So grey reads as a different material sitting next to the mould, with a hard line where they meet, rather than mould that is fading. That breaks the "a middle, not a yes/no" rule.

I built two versions that fade instead. They go by how long since that bit was fed, over about 6 seconds, and blend across a few cells where fed meets starved. They are behind F3 in the game. Grey is still the default until you pick. Each version is labelled only A, B or C below.

Which looks best: A, B or C?

What it costs

The faded versions draw about 0.3 ms a frame slower: 3.9–4.0 ms vs 3.6–3.8 ms on average, measured back to back. The game itself plays the same: drawing never touches the simulation. Running Dry still meets the star bar in all three.

To try them yourself: git fetch origin claude/project-thread-cek3ly && git checkout claude/project-thread-cek3ly, then cargo run --release --bin mould, and press F3 to cycle. The window title names the look.
