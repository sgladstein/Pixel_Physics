Mould prototypes, round 1 · the music one

Mould Organ

One food pile is the heart and three are bells. Every two seconds the heart thumps and a pulse runs out along the mould's tubes at 50 cells a second. Each bell rings when the pulse reaches it, so the length of a tube is the timing of its note. The question: is steering the network fun when the reward is a sound?

The timing is rock steady, and every tool changes what you hear. Left alone, no tube changed length by even one cell in 60 seconds, on 8 of 8 dishes. The lamp drags a note late while you hold it. A bridge between two bells adds an echo. A salt wall silences a bell until you lead the mould round it.

The catch: the mould straightens every tube again once you let go. A lamp push is gone 5 seconds after release, on 8 of 8 dishes. So the lamp works like a pitch-bend wheel and can't write a rhythm that stays. Whether that feels playful or frustrating can only be judged by playing it with sound.

Run it: cargo run --release --bin organ (sound on Windows and macOS). Branch organ-proto.

Does the timing wobble? 0 cells

Each dish was joined by an oats-only scripted hand, left alone for 20 seconds after the last oats were gone, then measured every step. Wobble is the longest minus the shortest tube distance from the heart to the bell during the window. The brief's bar: under 5 cells the timing reads, over 15 it is mush.

On that ruler, a lamp that visibly bowed the high tube moved the note 0 ms on 7 of 8 dishes. The organ measures straight-line length instead. That ruler sees the push and still reads 0 wobble. Cost and checks

Frame cost

The window's frame (two steps and a draw) averages 2.86 ms, with p99 7.5 ms and worst 17.4 ms, over 31,680 frames. Frames that contain a beat, when the pulse measures every tube, average 5.6 ms with worst 13.9. The mould game runs at 2.6 ms, worst 17. Tests 8 of 8 pass and clippy is clean. The Windows sound code compiles but has not been heard on a real speaker.

What I'd do next

  • Play it with sound for five minutes. The numbers show it keeps time and every tool is audible. Whether a groove that drifts back is a toy or an annoyance is a call only playing can make.
  • If the drift is the annoyance, add a lever that lasts: a tube that keeps its route after being held for a few beats, or salt that steers a tube as it grows instead of cutting it.
  • If the drift is the charm, lean in. Make it an instrument you hold, where the puzzle is keeping a rhythm while the mould pulls back.
  • Polish: an option to snap notes to sixteenths, a pentatonic scale so more bells always fit, and more than three bells.
