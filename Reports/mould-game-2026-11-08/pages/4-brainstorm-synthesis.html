Where Mould goes next

You asked what would make the slime mould demo a real game: who you are, what you're trying to do, what's out there, and what challenges could go in soon. Five brainstormers came back (theme, out-there, near-term challenges, toy feel, and real-world research). This page is what they agree on, each one's best pick, and the one choice I need from you.

Your call: which theme should the game-design brainstormer build on?

I recommend The Wanderer: you keep one named mould alive and lead it across a run of dishes, from a kitchen table to a forest floor. The food under it runs out, so it has to move on. The score is how much of it arrived. The other options are below. I'll put the choice on a card in the thread.

Where all five agreed, without being told to

Food should be seen moving. A pulse that rides out along the tubes from each food pile. It makes the mould look alive when still, shows which branch is starving (its beat stops), and is the real organism's own heartbeat (cytoplasm streaming back and forth).

Cutting off part of the mould does almost nothing today. This one is a surprise. A snip heals in about half a second, and because all the food goes into one shared store, a piece cut away still gets fed. The slow grey death the game was built around almost never shows.

Score an amount, not stars. "1,140 of 1,500 arrived" or "three sprouts flowered, one in leaf" is a spread. Three stars are three yes/no answers.

Don't slow the mould down. The lamp answers in 0.05 s and oats in about 0.5 s, which feels like touch. The best moments are over in a quarter second, so make them linger in the drawing and the sound, not in the sim.

Each brainstormer's best pick

Theme: who are you, and why care?

The Wanderer

You keep a pet slime mould (a real hobby: people keep Physarum on agar and feed it oats). Each dish, the pile it sits on drains in about two minutes and its scent fades, so the mould gets restless. You lead it to an exit with the lamp and oats. Tubes left behind go grey and die back, so the cost of moving is graded: a thin trailing line, or half its body. Its size and shape carry over to the next dish.

Runners-up: Lifeline (sprouts that can't eat on their own; your mould is their supply line, best folded into some Wanderer dishes) and Little Railways (villages light up when joined; clearest goals, but better as a puzzle pack). Ruled out: spread and conquer, because each pile feeds only about 300 walkers, so a mould that covers the map starves. A pet with hunger meters hides the best feedback the game has, which is the colour of the mould itself.

Cheapest test: swap the 90 s clock for an exit on the right edge, a Go key, and "Mabel arrived with N of M".

Near-term challenges for the demo

Eight hand-made dishes after the lesson

Build these three first:

  • Behind the Wall. Food behind a salt wall. Oats aimed straight at it drag the mould into the wall, so you have to lead it round.
  • Beat the Ruler. Three foods in a big triangle and a dotted line showing the shortest food-to-food tubes. Win by using less tube, which you can only do with a meeting point in empty space.
  • Running Dry. Every pile runs out in about 35 s and new food turns up elsewhere. The mould has to keep moving house. This fills the empty middle minute.

Also: Three Oats, Overgrown (trim with snips), Storm Season (three storms), Feed the Sprout, The Rival. Shared work is a level file, a "why it failed" line, and a dish select screen, about 3 days. Bar for each dish: doing nothing earns 0 stars, random play at most 1, the intended play 3. Running Dry is the Wanderer in miniature.

Toy feel: make touching it better

Pulse, snap, and a wound that stays open

  1. A pulse along the tubes, drawn from the feeding search the game already does. About 30 lines, well under 0.1 ms a frame.
  2. A "snap" when a bridge or loop closes: a flash at the exact cell where it joined, and a chime. Your favourite moment becomes an event the game can see and count.
  3. A snip that stays open about 4 s, drawn as a real slash with recoil and a tearing sound, so cutting delivers something.

Also found: every tube looks the same width, a trunk feeding three piles and a spur to one oat. The storm's cut ends recoil like a snapped rubber band, which is a lovely moment nobody advertises.

Really out there

Mould Organ

A pulse runs from the mould's heart along every tube, and each food node rings a note when it arrives. Tube length is timing, so bending a tube with the lamp bends the rhythm, and a loop makes an echo. It's a toy before it's a game, which is how you already play this.

Step 0 before any sound: measure how many cells the heart-to-node distance wobbles over 10 s. Under about 5, the rhythm reads; over about 15, drop it. Also ranked: Dry Rot (a fungus inside a timber tower in the full world; you choose where it breaks and falls; big payoff, costly build), Tug (two moulds, versus or co-op), Desire Lines, Constellations, Root Bridge, Grow a Brain.

Research: real slime mould, other sims, other games

Tubes that remember

In our sim a tube is just trail, forgotten in about a second. A real tube has walls that thicken over minutes when food flows through them and last for hours. Adding a slow "wall" layer would make trunks fatten over a dish, leave a ghost the mould regrows along after a storm, and make "I built that bridge and it's still there" true.

Next borrows: the heartbeat pulse; the mould learning the storm's rhythm and flinching before it comes (Saigusa 2008); light as a cost rather than a push (it takes a longer path to avoid sun); two foods to balance; a starved patch drying to a crust you can rescue; fusion with a rival; fruiting toward your lamp at the end. No goal-driven game uses a real slime mould sim yet. Sources were search summaries, since full papers were blocked here.

Whatever you pick, I'd build the toy-feel trio next (pulse, snap, wound that stays open). All five brainstormers point at them, none depends on the theme, and they fix two things you hit in your play: not knowing what snip did, and not seeing what food was doing.
