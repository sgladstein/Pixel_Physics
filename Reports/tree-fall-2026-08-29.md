# The fall: rotation, and an outcome the load model did not have

**Status: shipped and measured; the half that matters most is with the
owner.** The mechanism fires everywhere it should, and per piece it moves the
settled orientation — 28% lying to 36%, pieces left standing on end 364 to
287, over ten paired scenes (§4b). Whether the *pile* now reads as a felled
tree is an eye question, it is on the review board (§8), and no number here
answers it. **Read §4 before quoting §3**: the single scene is one sample from
a wide distribution and it flatters the change.

This is `Reports/plant-mechanics-handoff-2026-08-29.md` §5.1 built. The plan
of record is `Reports/tree-mechanics-plan-2026-08-29.md`; the handoff is what
happened between the two.

---

## 1. The number that reframed it

`filmstrip scene=fell fell=7150`, the shipped felling scene, settled at frame
8,200:

```
quarter turns: 0 asked, 0 refused by the fit probe (0%)
```

**Not one body rotated, in the whole run.** The rotation mechanism has
existed since M8, it is documented at length, its fit probe was repaired in
August after being found vacuous — and on a falling tree it has never once
executed.

The arithmetic says why. `ChunkBody::spin` accrues at `SPIN_PER_SPEED`
(0.012) per unit of speed per frame, and it turns at 1.0. The census on that
same run reports the **fastest piece at 3.30 cells/frame** over a drop of
about thirty cells, so the whole fall banks `30 x 0.012 = 0.36` of a quarter
turn, on top of a promotion seed of at most 0.6. Nothing ever reaches 1.0.

That is the handoff's §3.1 restated as a counter: what has always *looked*
like a crown collapsing was foliage running downhill, because a severed piece
had no way to travel and no way to turn. A trunk cut through at the base has
no speed at all in the frame it comes free, and every reason to go over.

## 2. What was built

Three things, and the handoff is right that they cannot land separately: a
rate with nothing to seed it is inert, a seed with only one handedness is
wrong half the time, and a tipping test with no outcome to express is
`open-bugs-handoff.md` §Q's measured failure.

### 2a. The rate, seeded from the break

`ChunkBody` gains `spin_rate` (quarter-turns per frame, signed) and
`spin_accel`, seeded at promotion by

```
alpha = g * sum(m*d) / sum(m*r^2)
```

over the piece's own cells about `Failure::at` — `d` the horizontal lever
arm, `r` the full distance, `m` the cell's material density. **Nothing
tuned.** For a uniform limb of `L` cells breaking at one end the sums are
`L(L+1)/2` over `L(L+1)(2L+1)/6`, so `alpha = 3g/(2L+1)`, converging on the
textbook `3g/2L` for a rod about its end. A bole comes over about once across
a fifty-cell drop and a twig tumbles several times in the same fall.

**It self-limits, and the parallel-axis theorem does it.** `sum(m*r^2)` about
the break is `I_com + M*D^2`, so a compact fragment `D` from the break gets
`g/D` — it is on the end of a long lever and swings slowly — and one *at* the
break gets `g*M*D/I_com`, which goes to zero with `D`. The maximum over `D`
is `g/2k` for gyration radius `k`, so a small piece can tumble fast and a
large one cannot, and nothing can be seeded into a spin its own size cannot
express.

Seeding from the *breaking torque* instead gives `spin` proportional to
`m*d`, so the heaviest piece spins hardest — backwards, and the thing
`SPIN_PER_SPEED`'s own doc records being tuned away from.

Held for the whole flight rather than applied once, because a piece that
breaks off is still being turned by its weight about the joint that gave way;
a felled trunk pivoting on its stump is exactly that. §5 records what that
simplification cost and how it is bounded.

### 2b. A signed turn, about the piece rather than its corner

`BodyCell::rotated` had one handedness. The handoff (§3.5) costed the reverse
at "three forward turns and a fit probe on each intermediate pose"; `Turn::Ccw`
is the exact inverse permutation instead — one turn, still exact on a grid,
no intermediate pose to check or to leak material through.

And `rotate_quarter` pivoted on the **body origin**, which `promote` places at
`cells[0]`: a corner, after the sort. For the 25x35 bole this scene produces
that swings the far end some fifty cells in the frame the turn takes, which
`rotation_fits` cannot see — it checks the final footprint, not the swept
path — and which mostly just gets the turn refused, because a pose fifty
cells sideways is a pose inside the hillside.

**The pivot is fixed at promotion and never recomputed**, and that detail is
load-bearing. Deriving it from the current offsets each time floors the mean,
so a body whose true centre sits at a half-cell comes out of a turn with its
floored centre a cell away, the next turn pivots about *that*, and the piece
walks. A clockwise turn followed by an anticlockwise one left a six-cell L a
cell from where it started — a position leak with no ledger to catch it. The
guard `the_two_turns_undo_each_other` found it before it shipped.

### 2c. The topple, which is an outcome and not a test

`open-bugs-handoff.md` §Q had already measured the tempting version: let
`load::bearing_moment`'s clamp reach `log` and the pieces are **crushed**,
not laid down (settled lying/upright 3/8 to 1/11, `log` 833 cells to 716,
`deadwood` 553 to 645), because the load model's only verdict for a thing
that fails is `breaks_into` — convert where you stand.

So the middle-third test lives in `rigid` now, on the far side of the flight,
where the piece is still a body and going over is something it can do. A body
that has stalled out is asked, once, whether its mass-weighted centre lies
outside the middle third of its footing — every cell with something under it
that is not itself and not a fluid, which is `bearing_moment`'s own reading
of a contact. If it does, it turns that way instead of settling. **Nothing in
the load model changes.**

It terminates three ways. A turn that does not fit is refused and the body
settles as it stands, wedged, which is a legitimate resting state. A turn
that fits usually lays the piece flatter, and a flat piece's centre is inside
its own footing. `MAX_TOPPLE_TURNS` (4) catches the remainder, because two
poses can each read as unbalanced in the other's favour and no argument from
geometry says they cannot.

## 3. What it does on one scene

`scene=fell fell=7150`, settled at 8,200, against the same scene before:

| `FALL=` | off | on |
|---|---|---|
| quarter turns asked | **0** | **3** (1 refused) |
| landed out of balance and went over | 0 | **11** (7 refused, 63%) |
| pieces lying / upright / square, per piece | 26 / 32 / 1 | 33 / 20 / 3 |
| **lying share** | 44% | **59%** |
| settled *clusters* lying / upright / square | 5 / 5 / 1 | 2 / 2 / 0 |
| `log` cells | 680 | 759 |
| `deadwood` cells | 606 | 543 |

Twelve of the thirty-two pieces that used to end up standing on end do not,
and more of the crown survives as `log` rather than crumbling to `deadwood`
— the opposite of what §Q's clamp experiment did to the same scene.

**The topple does most of the work here and the in-flight turn does little**,
which is the division of labour §2a predicts rather than a disappointment: on
this scene the biggest piece is the bole, its mass sits directly over the
cut, so `alpha` is near zero and it comes down more or less straight. What
lays it over is the tipping test, on landing. 11 topples against 3 turns is
that sentence as a number.

An earlier revision of this table read 22 turns and 16 topples, on a tree one
plant-line commit older and with rock seeded as well as tissue. Both of those
changed underneath it; see §4c.

**No frame timing from a `scene=fell` run is quoted anywhere here, and the
reason is a free positive control this package fell over.** Nothing is
promoted at all before the cut — both arms report `0 bodies` at frame 7,100 —
so the two worlds are *identical* up to frame 7,150, and every frame before
it is the same work twice. An early pair of runs nevertheless reported worst
frames of **49.45 ms and 28.65 ms**, at frames 5,353 and 6,838, **both before
the cut**: a 1.7x difference on byte-identical work. `CLAUDE.md`'s "a timing
number is only as trustworthy as the box was quiet", handed over for nothing.
The frame-cost measurement that *is* quoted is §4d's, on `scene=ants`, paired
and alternating.

## 4. …and what it does on twelve, which is a different answer

The table above is one run, and a clean first result in this engine is
evidence of an artifact rather than of a strong effect. Swept over three
species and four weather phases, paired, one binary, `FALL=off` against
`FALL=on`:

| | off | on |
|---|---|---|
| settled **clusters** lying / upright / square, pooled over 12 scenes | 37 / 48 / 9 | 30 / 49 / 7 |
*(measured pre-merge, with rock seeded; the conclusion is about the instrument
and does not depend on either.)*

**The pooled cluster census does not move**, and if anything it goes the
wrong way. Every individual run's rotation counters go from silent to dozens
of turns, and per-scene the orientation split moves in both directions. (The
first pass at this compared the two *binaries* rather than one binary under a
switch, over nine scenes, and reported 31/38/8 against 27/40/7 — the same
answer, and §6 is why that comparison should not have been trusted either
way.)

**The instrument is the first suspect, and its own documentation convicts
it.** `filmstrip`'s `log_pieces` folds settled `log` into 8-connected
clusters, so two logs that land touching are one "piece" whose orientation is
the *pile's* rather than either log's — its doc records the largest "piece"
moving from 49x48 to 99x71 purely because the pile packed tighter. A
statistic over three-to-ten merged blobs cannot resolve what happened to
thirty pieces, and a mechanism that packs a pile *tighter* moves that
statistic against itself.

So a per-piece census was added — `FailureCounts::settled_lying` and its two
siblings, taken in `rigid::settle` from the body's own bounding box at the
one moment its extent is unambiguous — and the sweep re-run through it.

### 4a. …and a second thing had to be got out of the way first

The per-piece census is **cumulative over settle events**, so it is only a
census of *pieces* if each piece settles once. On four of twelve scene-arms it
does not: `Reports/open-bugs-handoff.md` §Z3, filed from this work, is a
settled 8-cell fragment being promoted, going nowhere, settling and being
promoted again — **about one body every five frames, indefinitely**. It
happens in the `FALL=off` control too, so it is not the fall's doing; what the
fall changes is when it starts. A contaminated arm reports a hundred-odd
settles for one fragment and its orientation share is that fragment's pose.

Its signature is a body still in flight long after a clean fall has settled
(a clean one is done by ~7,230 on a cut at 7,150), so the census is read at
**frame 7,700** and any pair where either arm still holds a body is dropped.
Two of twelve go, both `shrub`, both contaminated in the **control** arm.

### 4b. The measurement

Ten clean pairs — three species by four weather phases — one binary,
`FALL=off` against `FALL=on`, read per piece at frame 7,700:

| | off | on |
|---|---|---|
| lying / upright / square | 160 / 364 / 40 | 191 / 287 / 48 |
| **lying share** | **28%** | **36%** |
| **left standing on end** | **364** | **287** (−21%) |
| scenes where the lying share rose | — | **8 of 10** |
| quarter turns / topples, all twelve | 14 / 0 | 21 / **109** |

So: **about a fifth of the pieces that used to end up standing on end now lie
down**, in every species and at every weather phase but two, where it is flat
rather than reversed. That is a real effect and a modest one — not the 17% →
59% the contaminated arms suggested, and not the nothing the cluster census
reported.

**The topple does nearly all of it**, which is the division of labour §2a
predicts: 109 topples against 21 in-flight turns over twelve scenes. A
severed crown's pieces mostly sit over the cut rather than out to one side,
so `alpha` is small and they come down more or less straight; what lays them
over is the tipping test on landing.

**One caveat that survives the filter.** `shrub f7200` reports 98 promotions
in both arms against a clean band of 26-57, which looks like a §Z3 cycle that
had already ended by the frame the census is read at, so the in-flight test
does not catch it. It is flat in both arms (12% against 11%) and dropping it
as well moves the pooled figures to 32% → 42% — the same direction, slightly
stronger. The pre-stated criterion is kept rather than tightened after
looking.

### 4c. Scoped to tissue, and what that cost is measured

`angular_acceleration` is as true of a slab of stone as of a branch, and the
rock line has the same defect on record — `open-bugs-handoff.md` §Q's
`scene=worked` needles are rubble standing on end. It is nevertheless gated
to organism tissue (`rigid::is_tissue`), and the reason is `CLAUDE.md`'s: a
change that reallocates a budget you have not costed is not scoped, merely
started. Rock destruction's constants and its gates are calibrated against
how debris tumbles now.

It is costed far enough to say what extending it would take.
`a_disturbance_extent_licenses_the_wound_but_not_the_chain` — a radius-20
charge in a massif at TIGHT, paired against the identical blast with the
disturbance's extent forced to zero — **fails** with rock seeded, and the
`FALL` switch says it is this and not the merge:

| radius-20 charge at TIGHT, 600 frames | licensed arm | leashed arm |
|---|---|---|
| cells promoted, rock not seeded | 701 | 506 |
| cells promoted, rock seeded | 723 | **1,217** |
| cells shattered | 245 → 246 | 276 → **365** |
| stone left standing | 29,577 → 29,490 | 29,555 → **29,356** |

The **licensed** arm barely moves. It is the *leashed* one that doubles —
and everything it destroys is still inside the leash, `max_damage_reach`
holding at 16, so the chain rule is intact. What changes is that landed
debris in more poses gets re-judged and more of it comes away, which is §Z3's
shape arriving on rock. The guard inverts because it is phrased as "the
extent licence buys more than a point licence", and the point licence now
grinds harder.

That is a real question about the destruction line rather than a tuning
detail, and §Q already says the decisive census for it has to be run on
`scene=worked` before anything there is touched. So: not here.

### 4d. What it costs per frame: nothing measurable

`ascii scene=ants` — 153 live organisms, 12,000 frames — three alternating
pairs, same binary, same session:

| run | `FALL=off` mean | `FALL=on` mean | off worst | on worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.170 ms | 2.148 ms | 26.40 ms | 23.63 ms |
| 2 | 2.200 ms | 2.185 ms | 22.66 ms | 23.92 ms |
| 3 | 2.187 ms | 2.187 ms | **41.29 ms** | 26.05 ms |

Mean **2.186 ms against 2.173 ms**, faster with the change in two runs of
three and tied in the third. That is noise in the direction that flatters it,
and it is reported as noise: the honest reading is no measurable cost. The
shape is why — everything new is O(cells) at an *event* (`angular_acceleration`
runs once per promotion) or at a *settle* (`tipping_turn` runs once per body,
only after it has stalled out), and the per-frame addition is two float
operations and a comparison inside `advance`.

**The worst-frame column is not a result and is printed to say so.** The
control arm alone spans 22.66 to 41.29 ms — a 1.8x range on byte-identical
work — and the ratio `CLAUDE.md` asks for before quoting a worst does not
pin it anywhere near: `mean x frames` is 26 *seconds* against a 26 *millisecond*
worst, so this is an order statistic over twelve thousand comparable frames,
which is noise wearing a number.

## 5. The regression it found, and the control that named it

`spin_accel` is held for the whole flight (§2a), which is right for a piece
falling through air for a few dozen frames and wrong for one that never
lands. A raft breaking up mid-pond wound itself to `MAX_SPIN_RATE` and
**stayed on the surface**: 34 of 80 cells still standing at rows 84..100
after 600 frames, and identically after 3,000 — so wedged, not slow. That is
`an_unsupported_raft_sinks_through_a_deep_pond`'s own bug returning by a new
route, and the test caught it on the first run.

The fix costs no new constant: water takes the same share of the turn that it
takes of the weight, which is the `carried` term `drag_through_liquid`
already computes for buoyancy. Something barely denser than water barely
sinks and barely turns; stone keeps about three fifths of its rate per frame
and has stopped turning inside ten.

## 6. `FALL=off`, and why comparing two binaries was the wrong control

The first comparison in §3 is a **two-binary** comparison, and every
instrument added alongside a mechanism is missing from the arm it is being
measured against. That is how the first reading of this change went wrong:
the per-piece census existed only in the new build, so the comparison fell
back on a cluster-level statistic that provably cannot answer the question.

`FALL=off` puts a body back on the pre-2026-08-29 rule — no rate seeded from
the break, no tipping test on landing, and the origin pivot restored, because
a control has to hold *all* of the semantics fixed and not just the two new
mechanisms. Verified against the pre-change binary on `scene=fell`: every
physics line identical.

It also keeps these runs reproducible after the mechanism has been tuned past
them, which is the reason `SETTLE_AUX` exists in the same file.

## 7. What is not built

- ~~**The tree does not hinge on its stump.**~~ **Built in round two — §9a.**
  This was the limitation the owner then rejected the whole change over, from
  the picture alone, which is worth more than the entry: a known limitation
  written down is not the same as one the player will forgive.
- **`rotation_fits` still checks the final footprint, not the swept path.**
  Turning about the centre shrinks the sweep from the body's whole extent to
  its bounding circle, which is most of the practical problem, and does not
  remove it.
- **`scene=fell` is `trees: 1`.** Nothing yet tests a tree falling *into* its
  neighbours — hang-up, crash-through, a stripped neighbour — and the handoff
  (§5.1) is right that this is where the most cinematic behaviour lives.
- **Rock is untouched, deliberately and at a measured cost** — see §4c. That
  covers `scene=worked`'s needles twice over: they are rubble rather than
  bodies that ever flew, so §Q's candidate (2) stands regardless.
- **The refusal rates are high** — 40% of in-flight turns and 52% of topples —
  and are not investigated. They may be a pile with no room in it, which is
  correct, or a probe that is too strict, which is not.

> **1)** All the leaves still fall off the branch (which I have multiple times

**He was describing the code.** §7 above lists "the tree does not hinge on its
stump" as a known limitation; he found it from the picture alone, and it is
the reason everything in §3 and §4 measured a modest effect. `fell_severed_
tissue` runs the fragment ladder over the whole severed region *at the instant
of the cut*: **56 separate bodies exist before a cell has moved**. There was
never a tree to hinge, and `topple` was only ever acting on pieces that had
already come apart.

### 9a. The hinge

The fragments stay — they are what stopped a crown reading as sawdust. What
was missing is that they had no *shared* motion. Every fragment off one
severance now carries the same `Hinge { pivot, alpha, omega }`, and while it
holds one its velocity is the rigid-body `omega x r` about that stump rather
than its own ballistic fall. A piece high up the trunk travels several times
faster than one near the base; the assembly sweeps; the base lands first; and
`landed` releases the hinge on impact, which is where "the bottom branches
break off from the impact" comes from. Gravity is **replaced** while hinged,
not added — `alpha` is computed from gravity, and the stump supplies the
reaction.

**Two defects on the first build, and the rendered sheet showed neither.** A
hinge about the wrong point is indistinguishable from falling, which is why
this needed `HINGE_PROBE=1` rather than another contact sheet:

- **The pivot was `broke_at`.** `Failure::at` is one arbitrary cell of the
  region — measured here the crown's far *left* edge, with the centre of mass
  58.5 cells to the right of it and only 2.0 below. `r` was almost horizontal,
  so `omega x r` pointed nearly straight down. `cut_face` takes the region's
  lowest row at its centre of mass instead, and `r` becomes (58.5, **-26.0**).
- **A units error.** `Hinge` integrates radians; `angular_acceleration` was
  returning quarter-turns. It returns radians now and `promote` divides at the
  one place that counts quarter turns.

### 9b. The leaves, and the number that says how deep to go

`clings_to_wood` was in the build and working. It was **one cell too
shallow**: `on_a_branch` asked only the four cells immediately touching a
leaf, and a crown is mostly leaf resting on other leaves. Censused on the
settled pile (`filmstrip`'s *foliage by steps to the nearest wood*), of 1,122
cells:

| steps to wood | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5+ | none |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cells | **405** | 201 | 130 | 85 | 214 | 87 |

One step holds **36%**; two holds **53%** — the first depth at which "most of
the leaves stay on the branch" is true rather than nearly true. Two rather
than more because depth is how near this gets to `deadleaf.ron`'s recorded
dead end: every step freezes a thicker shell of foliage around each limb, and
at four or five a dense pile of mixed log and leaf is immobile wholesale.

Measured after the change, the settled distribution moves as intended —
foliage within two steps of wood **53% -> 74%**, and the tail that had run
five or more cells clear of any wood **214 cells -> 74**.

**And it gave back what the hinge cost.** Swinging lands harder: wood
surviving as solid `log` had fallen 732 -> 587 cells. With the leaves holding
on it is **772**, better than either, and `deadwood` crumbs 606 -> 544.

### 9d. …and the hinge was rejected in its turn. Read this before rebuilding it

Card `20260829T150002405Z-82ae3b` put the hinge up on its own, and
`20260829T153254150Z-fc5748` put it up with the leaf fix. **Neither got a
choice**, and the two comments together are worth more than anything measured
above:

> No. A still looks like everything breaking but because of an **invisible
> force pushing things around**, not because it fell and hit the ground.

> I still think you are trying to force a full motion and break up. If the
> gnome hits one branch only that should fall off. if the cut severs nothing
> then nothing happens. if the whole trunk breaks, it just detaches from the
> ground. If it is tall and skinny and detaches from the ground it should and
> if that created enough stress/force then branches break. **It seems like you
> are trying to create an animation and not a physics system that results in
> realistic damage.**

**"Invisible force" is literally what `Hinge` is.** It sets `v = omega x r` on
every fragment, every frame, from a number computed at the moment of the cut.
Nothing in the world causes that velocity; it is imposed. It produces the
right silhouette by construction and no correct cause, which is exactly the
failure the ethos names — a mechanic that is right on paper and wrong in the
hand. §9a's numbers are all real and none of them was the question.

**The model he described is causal, local, and is the plan of record's own
order**, which this work skipped:

| the rule | what it implies |
|---|---|
| hit one branch, that branch falls off | severance is already local; nothing else may fire |
| the cut severs nothing, nothing happens | already true |
| the whole trunk breaks, it **detaches from the ground** | **no fragment ladder at the cut** — one unsupported body |
| tall and skinny and detached, it goes over | instability from its own centre of mass, not an imposed velocity |
| that creates stress, **and the stress breaks branches** | breakage is a consequence of the impact, not authored at severance |

So the fall cannot be built before the stress model underneath it —
`tree-mechanics-plan-2026-08-29.md` §2 — because without a stress number there
is nothing for a fall to *cause*, and anything that looks like a fall has to be
drawn rather than derived. This report built stage 4 without stages 1-3, and
the hinge is what that produces.

**What survives.** The leaf fix (§9b) is causal and independent and stands.
`FALL=off`, `HINGE_PROBE=1` and both censuses stand. The hinge itself is
recorded in `dead-ends.md` with the condition its rejection depends on.

## 10. Freshness

Written 2026-08-29 on `claude/plant-mechanics-handoff-d4zo44`. Every figure
measured this session on this machine.
