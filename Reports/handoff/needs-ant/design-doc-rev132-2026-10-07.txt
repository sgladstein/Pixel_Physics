A needs-and-jobs ant: design for a second walk
 · 
Summary
This is a design for a second way for the lab ant to decide what to do. It is built beside the shipped ant and switched off, so nothing else is risked if it fails (Scott, 2026-10-06). Four independent reviewers attacked the first version; this one takes their findings in, and Adversarial review lists what changed.
The problem. Today a small brain decides when to act and about 27,000 lines of hand rules decide where. The walk takes the first matching pull, so hunger acts only when nothing else matched, and six vetoes let a job hold a starving ant. The one inward pull that shipped, the rest pull, collapsed two colonies of four and is off, though on top of the hunger-first switch it collapsed none (C1). And today's ~5% of ant-time "in the dug nest" is mostly the top of the door shaft: only about half a percent of ant-time is spent more than 10 rows down.

The design. Two needs, hunger and escape, that outrank every job down to a hard floor. Seven jobs counting idle, taken by response thresholds that are genes, each with a stimulus the job itself lowers. One steering sum over a fixed set of cue terms, weighted by the winning drive. Idle ants are pulled nowhere: they slow down near a depth that suits their job and age, read from their own memory.

Scents, taken seriously. A nest gas would not steer ants inside rooms. Sealed, rooms are flat (9% of nest cells readable). A test of letting the mound's loose soil pass air found it lowers the nest's air level and leaves the slope exactly as flat: readings rise only because the sensor reads a ratio, no ant that steps on a threshold follows it out, and most of the level still counts ants the reader cannot sense. Today's scent layers cannot carry such a smell either. So the design uses no gas and asks for no change to the world's physics: memory, touch, gravity, routes and the shipped larva sense.

What the review changed. It is a bigger job than a hook: the brain reaches about ten places in the tick, so the tick and the 1,900-line act are split first, behaviour-identically. Four steps had been tried here and failed (the rest pull, crowding-driven digging, steering toward brood, dropping soil when lean); each got a named trace or re-test, and all four are back (Checks). Two metrics measured the wrong thing, so time in the nest is now measured by depth, and rooms by shape.

How it is judged. Designed behaviours with traces; counters that the feared colony (sitting on food and laying) fails; a no-veto guard that checks outcomes; paired seeds. Colony size is a floor.

For Scott: six cheap checks come first, using switches and tools that exist. All six are done (Scott chose checks first, 15:59). Holding ants inside and steering toward hungry larvae crashed no colony on today's game, and each raised deep time; the soil-air test and the soil-drop census each removed a part of the design; the new metrics are in the scorecard; the local digging switch had too few ants deep to judge. Building waits on your yes, and I recommend stacking the tested switches on today's ant first (Open questions).

The walk is a ladder. chooser_step fills one pull slot with the first match: store trip, walk home to lay, nest-worker leash, soil out, back to the face, laden carry, then the hungry way out, the mound way out, the rest pull. Scouting, the only hunger-driven search, acts only when nothing above it matched.
Hunger is what is left over, not a need that competes. That is why a pellet holder can starve beside food (traced, findings 30, 31, 35). The audit found five more vetoes of the same kind: the door throttle, the lean no-dig, the heap cue at zero, FaceTrip's only, and a caste fixed by id.
Nothing live pulls an idle ant inward, and the one that did is off. Every live pull aims at the door, out, or the ant's last home contact, except a digger's walk back to its face. Three inward pulls were built: the rest pull (NEST_REST, along NestWay to the end of a gallery), the leash's deep form (to the chamber floor, off), and the nest keeper's home (dead-ends 1254). The rest pull shipped on 2026-10-05 and raised decisions below the founding ground from 8.3-10.3% to 9.3-12.3% (4 seeds, 200k); then two colonies of four collapsed after 230k (553 to 57 ants, and 586 to 17), and it went off the next day under the owner's rule until the deaths are understood. Why is only partly traced (creature.rs:12296). On top of the hunger-first switch it collapsed none of four colonies (C1). Today's 4.4-6.0% of ant-time in the dug nest (head below the old ground line) is 81-85% within 4 columns of the door and 10 rows of the surface; only 9-11% of it is deeper than 10 rows (measured, baseline seeds 1-4, 50-300k).
Patches stack. The heap cue blocked breakouts, so DOOR_REOPEN waived it at the door. Lean ants could not dig, so MOUND_OUT=dig let them in the mound. HUNGRY_OUT's way missed the mound, so MOUND_OUT=way was built. Each was a correct fix of a traced problem; together they are a rule set nobody can predict.
Why it grew this way. The brain cannot sense where anything is: it has no direction inputs, and the trail readings are zeroed for it under the chooser. Evolution gets about 17 generations in a 300k test run, with mutation off while measuring. So every wanted behaviour had to be written by hand, one symptom at a time.
Goals and non-goals
The goal is an ant whose behaviour comes from a few general mechanisms reading local signals, so that the designed colony behaviours appear without a rule per symptom. Survival is a base need inside each ant, never the score.
Designed behaviours it must produce (the lab goal, Scott 2026-10-03, and the standing ant goals):
Ants live in the nest: most ant-time underground in the dug nest, not on the mound or at the door.

Separate chambers: brood rooms and worker rooms apart, built by the ants' own digging.

Food organised apart from brood: stores the colony keeps and draws on.

Foraging from the player's food, with recruitment that follows supply.

Brood care: larvae fed where they lie, eggs laid where brood is kept.

Later: defence against rival colonies and predators.

Principles.
Local signals only. An ant acts on what it touches, smells, carries or remembers. A global count is allowed only as a labelled stopgap with its local replacement named (Scott, 2026-10-06).

Needs are base. In Scott's words, "hunger or other metrics of colony survival should be a more base need in the ant behavior and the ant itself should use that to override other rules" (2026-10-06). Here that is hunger and being trapped; whether rest is one too is open question 3.

One way to steer. Every direction an ant takes comes from the same weighted sum over a fixed set of cue terms, so a new behaviour is new weights, not a new code path. The terms are of several kinds (memory, routes, touch, gravity, one short-range scent), because most scents have no usable slope where the ant needs one (Scents and fields in depth).

Designed, then judged by trace. Each mechanism names the behaviour it serves and the trace that shows it working; colony size is a check, not the target.

Mechanism is code, policy is genome. Weights and thresholds are genes that start at designed values (owner ruling on the breeding plan, §2a).

Frame cost is a hard limit, and the world stays deterministic per build and seed.

Non-goals.
Not a neural network learned from scratch. In our generation counts it would not get there, and if it did it might find the colony that sits on the food and lays.

Not a change to world physics: soil, footing, sliding, liquids and plants stay as they are.

Not a change to the outdoor or held games, or to the shipped lab ant, until it has earned it (next section).

Not exactness: a field that is nearly right and cheap beats one that is exact and slow.

Kept separate from the shipped ant
The new ant is a second walk the lab can switch to, built in its own files wherever it can be, off by default, so if it fails nothing else is lost (Scott, 2026-10-06). The shipped ant, the nest lanes and their baselines do not change and never wait on it.
It is a refactor, not a hook (engineering review). The brain's outputs reach about ten places in the tick, not two: the step-or-stay roll, the forage drive and its pace, the launch, the tumble after a failed roll, act, the chooser's persistence, turn and noise, trail A and B laying, the synapse tax, the trace row, and the speculative pre-computation of every ant's brain (creature.rs:7575-8210, :21925). act is 1,899 lines that mix the mechanics (eat, cut, pick up, put down, share, lay) with the hand rules that gate them, reading six brain outputs, 20 switches and 10 random draws, and the functions the new walk needs are private. So before any new walk exists, slice 0 splits act into mechanics and gating, and creature_tick into the shared body and the decision, as a pure refactor that must pass the identity check on its own.
Built as a hook after all (slice 0, 2026-10-06). Seven live branches were editing act, so moving its code would have made each of them re-merge. Instead the new walk hands act the urges its drive wants (1 for the verb it means, 0 for the others, the brain's own for share and bite) and takes over the step-or-stay chance, the step and an after-move hook, so act's mechanics and gates run unchanged and the no-veto guard checks by outcome that no gate blocks a need (src/sim/creature/needs.rs).

Off means bit-identical, checked properly. With the switch at Shipped the game must match main frame for frame. Today's identity check compares only summary columns on the frames both runs share. It must also require full frame coverage, diff the maps and the ledger byte for byte (deeptrace already writes them), and run CI's ascii and acceptance plus one outdoor and one held smoke run, all against a fresh off run of main at the merge base rather than a fixed baseline. The new code takes no random draws while off; when on, its draws come from new slots keyed per seed, ant and frame, so they cannot shift any other ant's stream.
A decision tax. Not running the brain removes its synapse tax, about 0.025 J a decision, close to the species' whole 200 J budget over a median life of about 7,100 decisions (inferred from the species file and the baseline ledger). The new walk pays an equivalent tax, so starvation compares like with like.
Branch and landing. Work happens on its own branch; each finished slice lands on main switched off, so the branch never drifts far behind (branchcheck.sh BxF under 300). Nest lanes never edit its files; it touches theirs only at the shared edits above, each proven neutral by the identity check.
Who builds it. A separate thread, on Opus, started only on Scott's yes. No current lane is diverted.
How to abandon it. Revert the hook commits and delete the module. The split of act can stay if it is worth keeping on its own merits (it makes the shipped ant's acts readable). Because the shipped ant never changed, nothing measured elsewhere is invalidated.
Architecture
The new walk keeps the engine's body and replaces the decision. One tick, in order:
Sense. Only what the ant can know: its own energy, crop and jaws; what it touches (soil, loose spoil, brood, food, nestmates, open air); short-range scents summed from sources in reach, read once per decision and dotted with each heading; trail presence where a step would land; gravity and walls; and its memory: the home vector, the door vector (where it last stood in open air), its depth below that point, patience, and how long it has been still.

Needs. Hunger and escape, each an urge from 0 to 1 that rises smoothly with its deficit. Water and heat come later.

Jobs. Forage, haul soil, dig, nurse, store and lay, with idle as the default. Each job has a stimulus the ant senses, a threshold that is the ant's own gene, and a stimulus that doing the job lowers. One job at a time, with hysteresis so it does not flicker.

Arbitrate. A need whose urge passes the job's hold takes over, whatever the job or the load. A job's hold has a ceiling below hunger's urge at 30% of the species budget (200 J), and the need-curve genes are clamped so mutation cannot move that floor. This is the audit's no-veto rule built into the structure; the guard in Measurement checks outcomes, not weights.

Steer. The winning drive (a need, the job, or idle) sets weights on a fixed set of steering terms. Every usable heading is scored by the weighted sum, and the pick is the existing choose_weighted, whose randomness is intended. A kinesis term decides whether to step at all.

Act. The drive picks the act from what is in reach, calling the mechanics split out of act: eat, put down, dig, pick up, load from a store, feed, lay or fight.

Trace. Every decision of a sample of ants (chosen by id), and every decision near an event (hunger, a death, a job change), records the drive, the job, the strongest terms on each heading and the heading taken, so "why did it do that" is answered per ant (owner's rule, 2026-09-20). Tracing every decision of every ant would be about 30 million rows a seed.

What stays exactly as it is: the body, falling and footing, energy costs, the crop and digestion, sharing, eggs and brood, the dig cut and pellet physics, the trail planes, and every world rule. What is new: needs, jobs, arbitration, the decision tax, the steering terms that do not exist yet (gravity, walls, the door vector, depth), the trace columns, and the split of act into mechanics and gating. What the brain did that the new walk must now do itself: decide step or stay; set persistence, turn and noise; set how much trail A to lay; and pay a decision tax (Kept separate).
Why this shape. It is the response-threshold model of division of labour (Bonabeau, Theraulaz & Deneubourg 1996) with needs placed above jobs, feeding a steering stage much like the one the shipped walk already has. The real difference from today is arbitration: today the first matching pull wins and silences everything below it; here every term the winning drive cares about is added, and needs outrank jobs by construction. Two cautions from the review. The model's promise that the share of ants on a job follows its stimulus holds only for a stimulus the job itself lowers; dig (space grows) and nurse (fed larvae stop signalling) have that, forage does not, so it gets its own brake (Jobs). And the model needs thresholds that differ between ants; with mutation off every ant is the founder's copy, so each ant gets a small fixed jitter from its id.
Needs
A need is an urge inside the ant's own body that rises with a deficit and outranks every job, which is Scott's ruling that survival overrides other rules (2026-10-06). Two to start: hunger and escape.
Hunger. Its urge is a smooth ramp of the ant's own energy: 0 above hunger_onset (a gene, starting at 0.6 of the species budget of 200 J), 1 at hunger_full (a gene, starting at 0.25), smooth in between. There is no cliff like today's LEAN_LINE at 0.5. Newborns hatch at about half the budget (traced by the inside lane, 2026-10-05), so they start mildly hungry; today the hungry pull out takes them out on 58-68% of their first exits. What hunger does, in order:
Eats what it holds. Food it took to eat, or a store load in the jaws, is eaten before the ant walks anywhere, and kept until the ant is fed: food picked up to eat is never put back down at home. A forager's cargo is the one exception: at the nest it goes down on purpose, hungry or not, and a forager still hungry then takes food to eat (Scott, 2026-10-07; see the purpose row in Scott's needs and jobs).

Lets go of soil safely. A pellet in the jaws is packed into the tunnel wall where there is one to pack against. It is put down in a tunnel only where the cell left behind keeps the tunnel passable, and never in the mound's tunnels. Dropping where it stands is today's LEAN_FORAGE drop, which the history traced shutting lean ants into pockets: of 127 ants that starved with empty jaws over 200-300k, 105 died in one (dead-ends 1262). Nest building is building a wall-pack rule for the shipped ant; it is not on main yet.

Goes toward food it can sense: food at its head, crumbs, a pile within reach (summed from sources, the way larva_scent is).

Goes out, when inside: back along the door vector, up, and along the walls. Below the door row NestWay can stand in, labelled; in the mound, where most covered ants are, it gives nothing, and no tested "out" cue exists there, so the door vector and the walls carry it. "Up" counts only while it gains rows and fades when it gains none: a pull up with no give-up pinned ants on walls before (deaths up a wall 85 to 180, dead-ends 1200).

Gets onto routes by trail presence, with direction from the home vector: away from home when empty.

Begs from a nestmate it touches, with today's sharing mechanics.

Scouts when nothing else is sensed: today's scout term, scaled by hunger.

Escape. An ant is trapped when a need wants it out and it cannot get there: it is covered, and its progress toward out has stalled for a while. Progress is measured against its own memory: the distance back to its door vector's origin, or for an ant that has never been out, rows gained. All of that is the ant's own state; it never needs to know that a door is shut, only that it has been pushing at soil. Escape rises with the stall, faster when hungry, lets the ant dig at any energy toward the door vector's origin and up, and has its own give-up: when digging gains nothing, it turns along the walls. Its nearest shipped ancestor, MOUND_OUT=dig, is on main and already took shut-in spells ending starved from 1.3% to 0.5% (dead-ends 1264), so that is the bar. DOOR_DIG's heap form also cut door-shut samples from 58 to 39 of 192, at a cost in colony size (dead-ends 1232).
Rest is not a need here. Idle is what an ant does when no need or job passes its threshold. Real colonies keep many inactive workers, which act as a reserve labour force (Charbonneau, Sasaki & Dornhaus 2017, PLoS ONE 12:e0184074, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0184074, via PubMed). Where idle ants sit is set by the depth kinesis in The steering law, not by a pull. Scott's ruling names hunger and survival; whether rest should also be a need is open question 3.
How a need takes over. Each job carries a hold, which rises while the job makes progress and fades when it does not, up to a ceiling below hunger's urge at 30% of the budget. A need wins when its urge passes the hold. The job is suspended, not forgotten: a digger that went out to eat walks back to its face by memory afterwards. The need-curve genes are clamped so that no mutation can move the 30% floor under the ceiling.
Later needs: water, once the lab has dry spells; heat.
Jobs and response thresholds
Each job has a stimulus the ant senses, a threshold that is its own gene, an end, and a way that doing the job lowers its stimulus. A job is taken with probability s² / (s² + θ²) per decision, where s is the stimulus and θ the threshold (Bonabeau, Theraulaz & Deneubourg 1996). Ants with low thresholds take a job first, and the share of ants on a job follows its stimulus only when doing the job lowers it.

Thresholds differ between ants. Today one ant in four is a nest worker for life, chosen by its id. Here thresholds are genes and shift with age: young ants start with low nurse and dig thresholds, old ants with a low forage threshold. Tracked colonies show workers moving from one group to the next as they age, with spatial fidelity organising the colony (Mersch, Crespi & Keller 2013, Science 340:1090, doi 10.1126/science.1234316, via PubMed); which jobs go with which age here is our choice. With mutation off every daughter is the founder's exact copy, so each ant also gets a small fixed jitter on its thresholds, drawn once from its id: development, not heredity, and invisible to the shipped walk.
Each job has a depth. Each ant carries a preferred resting depth, a gene shifted by age and job: stores shallow, brood care deep, foragers shallow, where returning foragers are met. Real nests zone this way, with stores near the top and brood deeper, set by microclimate (the project's nest-biology report, §3.2 and §5.4), and Cox & Blanchard's model puts different groups in different regions of the nest's air (2000). This is the design's only mechanism for designed behaviours 2 and 3, and it is untested.

The steering law
Every heading the ant could take gets a score, and the walk picks one with choose_weighted, as the shipped walk does:
score(heading) = persistence × route hold + Σ weight(drive, term) × term(heading)
The shipped walk already has this shape: its persistence is multiplied by 1 + 3 × trail presence, and the home, away and scouting terms are added to it (how-the-ant-works.md §6d). What is new is who sets the weights: the winning drive. Hunger weights the "out" terms, nursing the larva sense, idle almost nothing. Which terms exist is fixed in code; how much each counts for each drive is a gene. Separately, a kinesis decides whether to step at all.

The kinesis: step or stay. The shipped step roll is already a kinesis: the brain's Move falls with Crowding at −0.3 (how-the-ant-works.md §4). The new walk starts from that and, for an idle ant, adds depth: the chance of stepping falls as the ant nears its preferred resting depth (Jobs), and falls further with resting nestmates it touches. In the first build, depth is a labelled stand-in, rows below the founding ground. Its local replacement is depth below the door vector's origin, or, for an ant that has never been out, rows of packed ground overhead. Two cues that look natural are ruled out by measurement. Cover (under_cover) is true in the spoil mound, where 4 to 17 times more ant cells sit than in the dug nest. And sealed nest-air level is the same within 5% everywhere past the throat and scales with how many ants are inside, so a set point on it is a disguised census (Scents).

The gains already shipped keep their values (HOME_GAIN 1, TRAIL_GAIN 3, AWAY_GAIN 1, scouting 2, the door reader's FOOD_TRAIL_GAIN 6). They are also the evolved founder's real gains: each is multiplied by one of six walk genes (WALK_GAIN_SLOTS) that ship at allele 0, a factor of exactly 1, and the evolved founder sets none of them (code-read, lab/scene.rs LAB_ANT_TRAITS). New terms start at 1, the size of the home pull, and are switched on one at a time.
Where no cue exists, a term is 0, never a guess. No presence, no scent in reach, no door vector, or a sensor sample landing in soil all give 0. With every cue silent, persistence and the pick's noise make a correlated random walk, which is the scout.
Why this is harder than it sounds (Scott's point, 2026-10-06). A weighted sum has known failure modes, and each needs its own defence:
Terms that cancel trap the ant. Up against a door vector that points down, or a larva across a wall, can leave no heading that gains. So each term says what progress means for it (for up, rows gained; for a vector, distance closed) and fades when the drive stops making progress, while walls and noise rise. That is the shipped patience, which already works this way for the laden ant and the scout. Escape, which takes over when hunger stalls, has its own give-up, so the drive that takes over does not push at the same wall. A pull up with no give-up has pinned ants before: deaths up a wall rose from 85 to 180 (dead-ends 1200).

A slope exists only where a field has one. A sealed nest's rooms have none, and today's planes cannot carry an in/out smell (Scents). So room behaviours use depth and touch, and every gradient term says where its slope was measured.

The first ants to stop set the pile. Idle ants slowing beside resting nestmates can pile up wherever the first few stopped, the door included. The depth term exists to prevent that, and the first check of the new walk is where idle ants stop, by picture and by depth.

Saturation silences a term, and the obvious fix needs a partner. Trail presence over the mound is 0.98-0.99 on every heading, so the route hold scales every heading alike there and steers nothing. Counting presence above the weakest heading fixes that. Alone, that floor cost the colony its meal, because carriers lingering at the heap were what fed it (dead-ends 1258, 1260). Paired with filling the crop before leaving food, carriers brought more home, and what still killed colonies was the mound trap (1262). The new ant's carry home takes both parts: on top of the hunger-first switch, the pair did its job on all four seeds (Nest building, 2026-10-06).

A job's hold can outvote a need. A hold with no ceiling lets a digger in a good cut out-score hunger at 30% energy, which is today's veto rebuilt from weights. The ceiling sits below hunger's urge at that floor, and the guard checks that the ant ate or reached air, not that a weight points the right way (Measurement).

Weights can become the new rules. Eleven drives by fourteen terms is the same hand-tuning moved into a table. Each drive uses at most five terms, starting weights come from gains already measured, and the robustness score in Measurement flags any weight a result hangs on.

Every term carries a physics card before it ships, with ten lines: who writes the cue; whether its slope was measured where it is used; its read threshold against the storage step; where the sensor samples; what happens when the ant writes what it reads; and five lines the physics review added because each one moved an answer: where outside is (the sink); the field's time constant against how often it is refreshed; line of sight from the nose to the sample; how a reading becomes a term (sign, ratio or log, and its scale against persistence 1); and its dynamic range against the number of sources. Memory, gravity and touch terms fill in only the lines that apply. A term without a card does not ship.
Scents and fields in depth
"Go up or down this scent" is only as good as the scent. This section gives the evidence in three parts: what the trail loop already taught us, a measurement of whether a nest gas would have a gradient in today's nests, and each cue the new ant would use, with what it can and cannot steer. The physics review re-ran the measurement and found the answer hung on whether loose soil passes air; a follow-up test of that (C6) found it does not change the answer. This section gives both.
The upshot: the first build needs no new field. No nest gas would steer ants inside rooms: letting the mound's loose soil pass air lowers the nest's level but leaves its slope as flat as sealed, and the level mostly counts bodies the ant cannot sense. So the first build steers by memory (the home and door vectors), gravity, touch, routes and the shipped larva sense, and places idle ants by depth rather than smell. Nest air is dropped from the plan, and the world's physics stays as it is.
What the trail loop taught us
From the repo's own measurements, surveyed 2026-10-06. The full list with a source on every claim is needs-ant/trail-lessons-2026-10-06.md.
Who writes a field sets its slope. Only laden ants walking home lay the food trail, so its fresh end is the nest end: it read +0.04 to +0.23 toward home along the whole route. An ant climbing it walks home. The test bed's hand-laid ramp rose toward the food, so a "climb the scent" reader would have passed the bed and failed in the game.

A trail has no direction. Presence reads the same both ways along a route. Direction came from the ant's own sense of home: an away-from-home term took ants across the lattice scene 24 of 24, at a median 297 decisions, against 21 of 24 at 1,189.

An ant's own mark blinds its nose. The mark lands at its head, so "here" is always its freshest deposit: facing home read positive on only 1.2-8.2% of laden ticks. Making the plane four times more readable moved round trips 47 to 48. What worked was a path-integration input: completed returns 6.8% to 28.1%, better on 8 of 8 seeds.

The blend, not evaporation, sets a trail's life. On a one-cell line the 3x3 blend takes 16.7% a pass against decay's 2.9%. An unreinforced trail lived 144 frames against a 2,200-frame round trip: "a live map of where ants are, not a memory" (pheromone.rs).

Storage runs out at the faint end. On 8 bits the reading was exactly 0 at 0.7 and 0.9 of the way out. Sixteen bits took trail life from 144 to 1,476 frames.

Saturation erases information. Over the spoil mound trail A reads 0.98-0.99 on every heading, so the trail hold multiplies every heading about four times and the pull home loses: 84-89% of carries lost the pull home at least once. At the door, presence read 0.99-1.00 whatever the news.

Where the nose samples matters. The 6-cell sensor landed in sky or rock on 6 of 8 headings. The honesty gate and row projection took closed laps from 144 to 376.

Ants reading a field they also write change the field. Wiring "scent rising under me" into stepping tethered empty ants to the nest (reached food 199 to 156). A two-point reader changed the trail the ants then laid (+0.06 to -0.016). The repo's rule: size any change to how ants read a field against the field they will then make.

Lifetime is wrong both ways, and depends on the food patch. Too short and no road forms. Too long and dead side paths stay lit: food 104,340 to 82,820, lower on 8 of 12 seeds. Stale trails recruit to food that is gone, which a 700-frame news window fixed.

Instruments lied three times: a polarity metric that counted one shoulder of a blob, a filmstrip that never steps the planes, and an overlay that drew a 250-to-50 ramp as flat.

The planes ignore terrain. Scent spreads into rock and sky and leaks between tunnels. Nobody had measured how much that matters. The next part does.

What it shows:
Where outside starts moves the level, not the slope. My first check let gas escape only to open sky, so the spoil mound's covered passages sat between the nest and the outside, and rooms came out flat. The review found that letting the mound's loose spoil pass even 2% opens every map and nearly doubles readability, and that counting the whole mound as outside reads 74% of cells. The follow-up test (C6, 11 maps) found why, and it is not a slope: inside the nest the porous field equals the sealed one minus a constant, whose spread is at most 0.06% of the nest's span. The sensor reads a ratio, so a lower level reads better, and the sealed field read against a lower reference gives the same readings. The shape is unchanged (picture: needs-ant/porosity/porosity_s1_f200000.png). Cox & Blanchard's model nest gives the same plateau under sealed walls, "a plateau of high concentration in the back half of the nest ... and a steep linear gradient in the entrance tunnel" (2000, J Theor Biol 204:223, doi 10.1006/jtbi.2000.2010, via PubMed), but its plateau needs a long entrance tunnel, and here that tunnel is mostly the mound, which changes every few hundred frames.

Porous spoil steadies the level, and a leaking ground misleads. With the mound's spoil passing 10% of the air, no map seals and no refresh jumps more than 25%, against 43 of 101 maps sealed and 19 of 43 jumps with sealed soil. But 82-89% of a room cell's level still comes from more than 6 cells away, so it stays a head-count. Letting the packed ground leak as well, even at 0.1%, sends 59-83% of descents to dead ends inside the nest. And the engine's own words point the wrong way for a literal rule: its natural soil is "loose tilth" and its spoil "tamped ground", so "loose soil passes air" taken literally would make the ground leak. Measured offline on 11 maps (C6, needs-ant/porosity/results.md).

Sealed, the level is flat, census-like and jumpy. 96-99.7% of nest cells sit within 5% of the maximum, and the door reads 92-98% of the deepest room. Sources within the sensor's 6 cells make only 8-17% of a cell's level, and ants in the mound make 32-65% of the nest's top, so the level mostly counts bodies the ant cannot sense. Re-solved every 1,000 frames it jumps a median 16% per refresh and 3.4-fold over 100k frames, far more than the 2-8% between the door and a deep room.

An ant with a threshold barely follows it. An exact follower walks out from 99.5-99.8% of nest cells; one that steps only on a 1% difference, as the sensor does, reaches the sky from 0-0.7%. Tunnels are as flat as rooms (84-92% unreadable); the readable cells are a cross at the top of the shaft. And a quarter to a half of those few readable reads look through soil into another gallery. Porous spoil does not rescue it: a 1% stepper reaches the sky from 0.2-3.4% of nest cells at any conductance, and one stepping on its own 6-cell sensor from none (C6).

Today's planes cannot carry an in/out smell. They have no sink and fade 0.03 a pass, so a plane remembers about 400 frames and reaches about 1.7 cells: a blur of where ants crowd, peaking in the mound and pointing out on about half the cells. Only without the fade does it look like a nest smell, and then it points out on 24-26%. Raising the deposit saturates 16-bit storage on 23% of nest cells.

The gas must connect cells the way the walk does. The walk passes diagonal corner gaps, because it checks only the cells it lands in, so a gas that exchanges only face to face calls a nest sealed while ants walk out. That happened on 43 of 101 seed-1 maps from 150k to 250k, and every such gap was in the mound. On one map (seed 4 at 200k) the door was shut and no exit existed at all, so escape cannot lean on a slope.

Solving is cheap; meaning is the problem. A direct solve is about 0.1 Mflop a refresh (inferred well under 1 ms compiled; the worst frame is unmeasured). But a field solved to steady state answers instantly, where the gas it models, stepped at the trail planes' rate, would take 4.5-5.1 million frames sealed and 1.2-2.2 million with porous spoil to come within 10%: 4-17 times a whole run (C6; the rate is assumed). That is why it behaves as a census. Any use would read it relative to the level just walked through, smoothed over 10k-100k frames, never against a fixed set point.

The shipped larva sense works, but its strength saturates. It is a vector summed at the head: each hungry larva's need, pointing at it, in a 13x13 box. An exact follower ends beside a hungry larva from every cell in reach, with 100%, 20% or 5% of larvae hungry, and points into soil on 0-4% of cells. But its strength near brood is 0.97-0.98 with all larvae hungry and 0.88-0.89 with a fifth hungry, so a five-fold change moves a lay brake by about 0.1. The brake reads the summed need instead.

What this means for the design
The first build needs no new field. Routes use presence with direction from the home vector, as shipped. Out uses the door vector, up while it gains rows, and the walls, with NestWay as a labelled stand-in below the door row. Nursing uses the shipped larva sense. Every gradient term says where its slope was measured.

Inside, idle ants are placed by depth, not by smell or dark. Depth below the door vector's origin is the ant's own memory: it differs between rooms where nest air, sealed or porous, barely does, and it counts no bodies. Ants do cluster by mutual attraction, and light's effect depends on caste and species: Lasius niger foragers cluster less under red light while its brood-tenders do not, and Crematogaster scutellaris cluster no less under it (Depickère, Fresneau & Deneubourg 2004, J Insect Physiol 50:629, doi 10.1016/j.jinsphys.2004.04.009; 2008, 54:1349, doi 10.1016/j.jinsphys.2008.07.013, via PubMed). Neither study places the cluster inside a nest. So touching resting nestmates slows an ant, depth decides where, and darkness is not a cue.

Real nests zone by depth without following a gas. In the Florida harvester ant, workers sort by age along the nest's depth, older ones higher, by their own movement and choice, while soil CO2 rises five-fold from the surface down (Tschinkel 2004, J Insect Sci 4:21, doi 10.1093/jis/4.1.21). Venting that CO2 gradient away, or even reversing it, changed neither the sorting nor the nest's shape (Tschinkel 2013, PLoS ONE 8:e59911, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0059911). Leaf-cutting ants, by contrast, do use CO2 to choose where to keep their fungus (Römer, Bollazzi & Roces 2017, PLoS ONE 12:e0174597, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0174597); all via PubMed. So zoning by a depth sense the ant carries has a basis, and a nest gas is not needed for it.

Routes stay presence plus direction from memory, as the shipped walk already does. Real ants also get direction from path integration (Müller & Wehner 1988, PNAS 85:5287, doi 10.1073/pnas.85.14.5287) and from the angles where trails branch (Jackson, Holcombe & Ratnieks 2004, Nature 432:907, doi 10.1038/nature03105), both via PubMed. No term here uses branch angles.

Escape never assumes an exit exists. Shut in means dig: up, and toward the last remembered way out.

No nest gas, and no change to the soil's physics. C6 answered open question 1: porous spoil moves the nest's air level, not its slope, and no conductance makes it something an ant can follow out or a local signal. Depth from the ant's own memory does the job a gas would have done. If a gas is ever built for another reason: mound spoil only, at 5-10%; the packed ground never leaks; cells connect as the walk does, corner gaps included; it is solved directly, since a stepped gas would not settle within a run; and it is read relative to the level just walked through.

Shared, not kept apart, for six slots. The new genes live in their own table, read only under the new walk, so the shipped ant's random stream and every off-arm run stay bit-identical. But the six walk gains are shipped slots that the new walk also reads, so once mutation is on, selection under one walk moves genes the other reads. That is fine within one run; it means a genome evolved under one walk is not a fair founder for the other.
Thresholds differ between ants even with mutation off. Mutation stays off while measuring (mutation_off_for_measuring()), so every daughter is the founder's copy. Each ant therefore gets a small fixed jitter on its thresholds and preferred depth, drawn once from its id: development, not heredity, and invisible to the shipped walk. Mutation is turned on only for an evolution round after the walk has passed acceptance; the weights and thresholds are then what evolution tunes (owner ruling on the breeding plan, §2a).
Age. Thresholds and preferred depth shift with age on a fixed schedule at first: young ants nurse and rest deep, old ants forage. Making the schedule a gene comes later.
Gene extremes are tested. Every clamp gets a unit test at both ends: an ant with the latest hunger onset and the highest hold ceiling still eats or reaches air in the guard's scenes (Measurement).

1. Today's headline number mostly counts ants at the top of the door shaft. "Time in the dug nest" means the head is below the old ground line. Of today's 4.4-6.0%, 81-85% is within 4 columns of the door and 10 rows of the surface, 55-61% is within 3 rows of it, and only 9-11% is deeper than 10 rows. Piling ants in the doorway raises the number; clearing the door knot lowers it. Report it by depth (for example, time more than 10 rows down and outside the door column) beside the headline.

2. under_cover is true in the spoil mound. It is a roof test (any soil within 20 rows straight up), so 89% of the ant cells it calls covered are above the ground line, in the mound. A rule keyed on it, or on inside_nest, treats the mound as inside the nest; dead-ends 1232 records a rule that never fired for that reason. Worth checking which hunger-first parts key on it.
Nest building
A finding; changes no code
Measured by three reviewers independently on the baseline maps; code at creature.rs:11915 and :12117
3. The room rule counts the shaft and merges rooms. The scorecard's room cells (7 of 9 neighbours open) find exactly one room on all 12 baseline maps, from 2 rows under the ground down to 28-50 rows, shaft included. Two chambers joined by a tunnel 3 or more wide count as one, and a wide stub can count as a room. "Separate rooms of 30+ cells" needs a chamber defined by shape, checked on a positive control: two carved 8x8 rooms joined by a 3-wide tunnel must count 2.

Scoring only
Measured by the engineering reviewer with the scorecard's own rule (roomshape.py)
4. The rest pull's best case was small. NEST_REST=workers raised decisions below the founding ground from 8.3-10.3% to 9.3-12.3% (4 seeds, 200k), then two of four colonies collapsed after 230k. A re-test (audit step 4) should be scored by depth (entry 1), and under the owner's rule it needs that collapse traced first. The partial trace found the starvers were pellet holders in the mound's tunnels; why food pick-up fell is untraced. Re-tested on top of hunger-first and scored by depth (C1, Nest race): no colony fell, and time deeper than 10 rows rose on all four seeds.
Nest building; re-tested by Nest race (C1)

Code-read (creature.rs:12296) and the rest pull's trace notes
5. The local dig-urge test is open and cheap. CROWDING_LOCAL=near came out a coin flip four times when scored on room totals (dead-ends 1878). Its own re-test condition, scoring on digbox's chambers and contrast columns, has not been run. It costs runs, not code, and it is the most direct test of audit step 5 (separate rooms). Given a place where ants gather it made the nest flatter, so run it without an inward rest rule.

6. Today's scent layers cannot carry an in-or-out smell. They fade fast: a smell's memory is about 400 frames and it reaches about 2 cells. So a smell laid on them is a blurred map of where ants are, peaking where they crowd (the mound, on seed 1), and its readings point out about half the time, which is chance. With no fade (trail A's setting) it points at the roof instead. An inside home smell cannot be built on today's layers.
Any lane proposing a nest or home smell
A finding; changes no code
Measured by the physics reviewer on 2 maps at today's constants (blend 0.25, fade 0.03)
7. A nest gas would not hold ants in rooms, porous soil or not. Sealed, a nest gas is flat inside rooms and its level is nearly the same everywhere below the shaft top. Letting the mound's loose soil pass air lowers that level but leaves the slope unchanged: readings rise only because a ratio sensor reads a lower level better, an ant stepping on its own 6-cell sensor reaches the sky from no nest cell, and most of the level still counts ants beyond its reach. Today's ant has no nest gas, so nothing changes now; a lane proposing a nest-air or CO2 cue should read needs-ant/porosity/results.md first.
Any lane proposing a nest-air cue
A finding
Measured on 11 maps (the gradient check, the physics reviewer's porosity sweep, and the soil-air test, C6)
8. The shipped larva sense steers well but saturates near brood. Followed exactly, it reaches a hungry larva from every cell within its 6-cell reach. But its strength at laying sites is 0.97-0.98 with every larva hungry and 0.88-0.89 with one in five hungry, so a five-fold change in brood hunger moves it by about 0.1. Laying keyed to larval hunger (audit step 6) needs an unsaturated input, such as the summed need.

10. doorseal.py's caveat about corners is unnecessary. The walk passes diagonal corner gaps: nothing checks the two corner cells of a diagonal step (body_after_step then landing_is_placeable_through_tissue). So the tool's 8-way flood matches the walk at corners, and its "open" verdicts are not inflated by them.

A docstring fix
Code-read by the physics reviewer and by me
11. Hold resting ants by slowing them, not pulling them. A fed nest worker with no job steps less often the deeper it is, and hunger switches the slow-down off. It adds no pull, so it cannot outrank hunger or walk a pellet holder into a pocket. C1 has since found the pull safe too once hunger always wins, so slowing's edge is now that it needs no map of the nest. The depth cue cannot be under_cover (entry 2): use rows below where the ant last saw open air, or below the founding ground as a labelled stand-in. Keep it to nest workers, because fed scouts must still scout: resting every idle ant starved the colony bed (births 22 to 5 per seed).
