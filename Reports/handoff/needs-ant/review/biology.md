### B1. Nest-air LEVEL is flat across the whole nest, so the idle kinesis cannot place ants anywhere inside, cannot separate food from brood, and is predicted to park idle ants at the throat
- Severity: BLOCKING (it is the doc's stated mechanism for designed behaviour 1, and the doc's own fallback for behaviours 2-3).
- Hits: *Each cue the new ant would use* ("Nest air, its level ... the level itself says how far in"); *What this means* item 2 ("This is the mechanism for 'ants live in the nest'"); Jobs table, Idle row; Risks row 3.
- Evidence (MEASURED: `python3 -I scratch/where.py scratch/gc` = the author's own `gradcheck.solve`, sealed walls, the author's sources, on the 7 maps whose sealed field is defined): cells of the dug nest at >= 95% of the nest's maximum level: s1/200k 828/845, s2/100k 536/553, s2/295k 1013/1035, s3/100k 534/537, s3/200k 784/788, s4/100k 581/583, s4/295k 1163/1169 (96-99.7%). Median level at brood cells 0.993-0.998 of max; at inside food (two maps that have any) 0.996 and 1.000, i.e. food sat DEEPER than brood (median 29 and 37 rows below ground against brood's 16 and 27). The level is an inside/outside flag, not a depth gauge: everything below the exit tunnel reads the same to within 5%. (The doc's own "room varies 3-9%" is the same fact seen per room.)
- Consequence (INFERRED from the measured shape): "slow and stop where nest air is high" switches on at the first cell where the level reaches the plateau, which is the inner end of the exit tunnel -- the throat every forager and hauler passes; the idle "inward by the nest-air slope" term has no slope past that point to carry them deeper. Resting nestmates as a stop cue then amplify whoever stopped first, which is the pile-up Risks row 3 lists, here predicted rather than possible. In slices 1-3 there is no nest air at all and the stand-ins are `under_cover` (binary) and the nest census (uniform over the nest, see B4d), so no kinesis input in slices 1-3 varies between places inside the nest either.
- Smallest fix: strike "the level itself says how far in"; state the measured flatness (>=95% of max on 96-99.7% of nest cells); name a local depth gauge that does vary between rooms -- the ant's own path-integrated distance or rows from its last open-air cell (the door vector's length, which the design already keeps) -- and make the preferred resting depth a per-ant gene shifted by age, so the slice-1 gate can be about WHERE idle ants stop, not only whether they are inside.

### B2. No mechanism in the design produces "separate chambers" or "food apart from brood"; every steering rule converges on the brood
- Severity: MAJOR.
- Hits: *Goals* behaviours 2 and 3; Jobs table rows Store, Lay, Nurse, Idle; *Measurement* rows 2-3; open question 3.
- Evidence: CODE-READ of the doc: Lay steers "toward brood, by touch and larva scent"; Nurse "up the larva scent"; Idle stops where nest air (bodies and brood are its sources) is high and nestmates rest; Store goes "toward food lying inside ... so piles grow where food is". Nothing anywhere pushes a store, a resting worker or a brood item away from anything. MEASURED (where.py, 12 maps): brood is 100% inside the dug nest on every map (e.g. s1/200k 305/305) and food or crumbs inside are 0-3 cells per map, so the store rule has nothing inside to grow from and the first inside drop -- which decides where the store is -- is unspecified (the Store job "ends when food put down beside food", which cannot happen when there is none). The repo's own biology (`Reports/nest-biology-2026-09-19.md` §3.2, §5.4) says chambers differ because of microclimate along the vertical axis -- stores shallow and dry, brood deep and humid, "food coming in stops near the entrance" -- and the design defers water and heat to "later" and has no depth-graded cue that works inside (B1). Two papers the doc cites say the same thing and are cited for something else: Cox & Blanchard's "regions of the 'dynamic template' correspond to regions occupied by different groups of ants" and "wall building occurs along lines of similar concentration"; Mersch et al.'s groups defined by spatial location. Open question 3 ("should resting ants also seek particular rooms, away from brood?") hands the missing mechanism to Scott.
- Smallest fix: either say plainly that slices 1-3 have no mechanism for behaviours 2-3 and gate only behaviour 1, 4 and 5 there; or add one named local mechanism with its trace: a per-job preferred depth (the door-vector length of B1) -- store drops shallow, lay and nurse deep, idle by age -- and a positive control scene where two depths exist and the metric must report two rooms.

### B3. The dig rule stops in exactly the regime that makes branches, and steers diggers apart, so it is not a second-room mechanism
- Severity: MAJOR.
- Hits: Jobs table, Dig row; steering weights "Dig | face vector 1, down 0.5, crowd at the step -1"; slice 2 gate "any second room".
- Evidence: the repo's own summary of Toffin et al. 2009 (`nest-biology-2026-09-19.md` §4.1, line ~520): "high density -> uniform digging -> round; falling density -> localised buds -> branching". The doc ends the dig job when "crowding at its face falls below threshold" and adds "crowd at the step -1, away from crowds", which removes the aggregation of diggers at a few sites that localises a bud. "Bodies per open cell within 2" also has the open cells as its denominator, so a lone ant at a narrow tunnel tip reads more crowding than ten ants in a room (INFERRED from the definition): the stimulus favours lengthening tips over widening a room. The rule-audit's L2 calls local density "the most direct lever on separate chambers" -- a lever, not a demonstrated room; nothing in the design localises a new cavity away from the first.
- Smallest fix: name the positive feedback that localises a bud (e.g. attraction to recent cuts -- the building/dig scent the doc already plans, read by the dig job, not only by drops), keep the job alive at low density where that cue is present, define crowding as bodies within 2 (not per open cell) or justify the ratio, and add a scene-level positive control for "a second room forms".
B1 addendum (MEASURED, `python3 -I scratch/throat.py scratch/gc`, same 7 maps): the level does rise monotonically inward (Spearman with walking steps out 0.77-0.89), but inside a 5% band; 40-95% of nest cells sit at >= 99% of max. The first cells at >= 95% are 9-22 walking steps from open sky and 1-5 rows below the ground line (median of the 10 nearest the door), against a nest median of 25-40 steps and a brood median of 26-43. And (CODE-READ, `gradcheck.solve` is one linear solve, c = A^-1 src) the absolute level is proportional to the total source, i.e. to how many ants and brood are inside: a fixed level set point is a disguised colony count -- in a small colony nothing reaches it, in a large one the shaft does. A level kinesis must read the level relative to something the ant carries (the level where it last rested, its own recent maximum) or use a different depth gauge.

### B5. "The only global knowledge left is the NestWay stand-in" is false: six more globals sit inside the "local" stimuli
- Severity: MAJOR (owner ruling: a global only as a labelled stopgap with its local replacement named).
- Hits: *No colony counts*; Jobs table (Forage, Haul, Store); steering table (door reader, kinesis); *Escape*; slice 1.
- Evidence:

The audit's own globals table lists this ("food distance from every door ... local version: `forage_max`"); the doc calls `met` "local and graded" and does not carry the replacement over.
  b. The door reader (gain 6, kept in "Forage, going out"): it acts only while the ant stands in `door_site`, a box defined from the nest-site registry (`how-the-ant-works.md:794-806`). An ant cannot sense that it is in the door box.
  c. "Inside" is used six times (Hunger inside/outside weights; Haul "loose soil at its head inside"; Store "inside the nest"; "food lying inside"; hunger "goes out, when inside") and never defined. The shipped `inside_nest` (`creature.rs:12117-12119`) is `under_cover` OR "in the shaft of `world.nearest_nest_site`" -- a registry lookup, added because `under_cover` reads "outside" in an open vertical shaft.
  d. Kinesis stand-in `Crowding`: at the nest it is the nest-room census, `2 / (2 + roofed empty cells per ant)` for the whole nest (`how-the-ant-works.md:251`; audit L2: "every ant reads the same value to four decimals").   e. Slice 1 wraps "the shipped rules" for haul and dig as jobs: the shipped dig urge is that same census (audit L2) and the shipped haul uses `SOIL_WAY`/`NestWay` maps.
  f. Escape's "progress toward out has stalled": the doc never says what progress is measured against. The candidates are NestWay distance (global), distance to the door vector's origin (undefined for an ant that has never been out -- most nest workers), or height (wrong wherever the way out first descends; it would make a hungry ant in such a room dig a new hole through the roof).
- Smallest fix: one locality table, every input with its code site, local or global, and its named local replacement (a: `forage_max`; b: "I just stepped from cover to open air"; c: cover plus rows below the door-vector origin; d: bodies within radius 2, the shipped "elsewhere" branch, `PIXEL_PHYSICS_LAB_ROOM=off`; f: say which), and strike the "only global" sentence.

### B8. With mutation off, every ant has the same thresholds, so the response-threshold division of labour the doc describes does not exist in any measured run
- Severity: MAJOR.
- Hits: *Jobs and response thresholds* ("ants with low thresholds take a job first"); *No fixed caste* ("thresholds differ between ants (genes)"); *Genes*; *Comparison*.
- Evidence: CODE-READ `src/sim/creature.rs:5802-5809`, `mutation_off_for_measuring`: "births inherit exactly". The standard setup is one evolved founder with mutation off, so every ant carries the founder's thresholds. What remains is the fixed age schedule and the per-decision coin of s^2/(s^2+theta^2). In Bonabeau's model the spread of thresholds between individuals is what makes specialists; here the only spread is age.
- Smallest fix: say so; if individual variation is wanted while measuring, add a deterministic per-ant offset (from the id, not the random stream, so the off arm stays bit-identical) and label it as development, not heredity.
### B9. The forage stimulus is a one-meeting plateau, not a rate, has no negative feedback, and (as shipped) never reaches an ant that has not foraged
- Severity: MAJOR (behaviour 4, "recruitment that follows supply").
- Hits: Jobs table, Forage row; *No colony counts* (Gordon 2013); *Why this shape* (Bonabeau: "the share of ants on it follows how strong its stimulus is").
- Evidence: CODE-READ `how-the-ant-works.md:937-944`: under `met` the drive is 1 for `RETURN_WINDOW` (1,400) frames x the ant's gene after the last meeting, then fades; one meeting saturates it, so one return per window and a hundred give the same stimulus. `how-the-ant-works.md:889-890`: the drive "reaches an animal that has foraged"; and "an ant that has met none counts from its `born_frame`", so if the design drops the foraged gate every newborn reads the full plateau for its first 1,400 frames. The doc says neither. INFERRED: in Bonabeau's fixed-threshold model "share follows stimulus" holds because doing the task lowers the stimulus everyone reads; of the design's stimuli, dig (space grows, crowding falls) and nurse (fed larvae stop signalling) have that; forage has the opposite (more foragers, more returns, more meetings) and its only brakes are a full crop and the ant's own hunger. Gordon's regulation (her wider work; the quoted abstract says only "a network of local interactions") is the RATE of returns, which a plateau cannot carry.
- Smallest fix: make the forage stimulus a leaky count of meetings with laden returners (a rate), say exactly who it reaches (foraged only, or everyone, with the newborn case), and give every job a line "what lowers this stimulus when ants do the job".

### B10. The idle reserve rests where the forage signal is never delivered

### B12. No act moves stored food to larvae, so "stores the colony draws on" and "young ants nurse" cannot both happen

### B14. The aggregation papers do not support "dark" as a stop cue, nor aggregation as the mechanism that puts ants IN the nest
- Severity: MAJOR (it is the cited basis for "This is the mechanism for 'ants live in the nest'").
- Hits: *What this means for the design* item 2; Kinesis row.
- Evidence (against the abstracts in the brief): Depickere et al. 2004 compares red light with total darkness; red light is used in ant labs because many ants see it poorly, so "brood-tenders aggregate even under light" overstates it. Its result is caste-dependent: foragers "show low assembly under red light", brood-tenders "aggregate well in both conditions". Depickere et al. 2008 says Crematogaster aggregated "just underneath" the red light and "the level of aggregation is not affected by the luminosity condition", which contradicts "aggregate in the dark" for that species; it supports inter-attraction only. Both are arena experiments: they show that ants cluster, not where they cluster, and nothing in either is about staying inside a nest.
- Smallest fix: "Ants cluster by mutual attraction (2004, 2008). Light's effect depends on caste and species: Lasius foragers cluster less under red light, its brood-tenders do not, and Crematogaster cluster no less under light (2004, 2008). Neither study places the cluster inside a nest; that is what our kinesis must add, and B1/B4 say its stand-ins cannot." Make the dark term's weight per caste/age, or drop it.

Both import the author's `gradcheck.py` unchanged (copied) and use its `load`, `masks`, `geodesic`, `avg_sources` and `solve(openm, sky, src, 0.0, 0.0)` (sealed walls, author's sources: ant 1, brood 0.25, averaged f-5k..f+5k).
- where.py: per map, ants and brood by region (dug nest = open, below row 60, reachable from sky; mound = open above row 60 with something over it; sky); inside food = `f` below row 60 touching a reachable nest cell, plus `c` in the nest; level L = c / max over reachable nest cells; zone L >= 0.95; median rows below ground of the zone, of brood, of inside food; median L at brood and next to inside food.
- throat.py: share of nest cells at L >= 0.99; walking steps out (`geodesic`) of the first L >= 0.95 cell, of the nest median and of the brood median; Spearman(L, steps out); rows below ground of the 10 L >= 0.95 cells nearest the door.
