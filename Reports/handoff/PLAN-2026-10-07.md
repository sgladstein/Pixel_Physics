## 1. Scott's goals

Stated 2026-10-03 and held since:

- **A lab colony with a stable population** that survives long term. Stable, not
  big. Smaller colonies are fine; booms followed by famine are the failure.
- **The colony lives in its nest.** Ants should spend their idle time inside the dug
  nest, not on the mound or the surface.
- **Separate chambers**: brood in one place, resting workers in another, **food
  stored apart from the brood**.
- **Foraging from a far food spot.** The player puts unlimited food in one place
  (player food never decays); the colony must fetch it from a distance.
- **All of it emerges from the ants' own rules.** No drawn rooms, no scripted
  building, no fixed castes where a rule could do it. Prefer rules that could evolve
  (genome or brain-weight dials over fixed constants).
- **Cite the biology.** Every rule about what real ants do needs a source; never
  answer "how real ants do it" from memory. Check the repo first
  ([`../nest-biology-2026-09-19.md`](../nest-biology-2026-09-19.md),
  [`../ant-sim-literature-review-external-2026-09-19.md`](../ant-sim-literature-review-external-2026-09-19.md),
  [`2026-10-07/inside-01-where-real-ants-are.md`](2026-10-07/inside-01-where-real-ants-are.md)),
  then search, then cite either way.
- **Evolving creatures** is the long-run aim; the lab is "the main game"
  (2026-10-02).

### 2.1 Trace before the next fix (Scott, 2026-10-06 22:56)

> "This should be your standard practice. You run your test and you deep trace it to
> understand the problem in full. If you don't fully understand what's going on,
> stop suggesting new fixes and implementing and testing them."

- After every test arm, trace the outcome down to individual ants: who, where, when,
  which rule acted on them, what they sensed.
- A message that proposes a next fix first says what is now understood and what is
  still untraced. If something is untraced, the next step is the trace.
- If logs can't answer a question, re-run **with more logging**, never with a
  changed rule.
- **The flow for any new rule:** trace, then a written proposal, then **a review by
  a second session** (an adversarial read of the proposal against the code and the
  trace), then build it **off by default**, then test. Worked examples of the
  proposal/review pair: [`2026-10-07/store-edible-proposal-2026-10-07.md`](2026-10-07/store-edible-proposal-2026-10-07.md)
  with [its review](2026-10-07/store-edible-review-deep-trace-2026-10-07.md);
  [`2026-10-07/way-over-ground-proposal-2026-10-07.md`](2026-10-07/way-over-ground-proposal-2026-10-07.md)
  with [its review](2026-10-07/way-over-ground-review-deep-trace-2026-10-07.md).
- Why: on 2026-10-06 lanes tried one fix after another on the nest problem without
  knowing why the last one failed. Scott: "failing at this over and over".

### 2.2 Keep failed experiments findable (Scott, 2026-10-07 19:12)

> "These sound like useful test or feature we may want to retest once we solve some
> of these issues ... I don't want all this deleted or lost in an old abandoned
> branch."

- Never delete or strand an experiment. Each one gets **a named branch**, a line in a
  **README catalog** on that branch, and a **`Reports/dead-ends.md` entry** with the
  condition its rejection depended on and when to re-test.
- The catalog of the ten parked nest experiments of 2026-10-03/04 is on branch
  `claude/parked-nest-experiments-2026-10-04`, file
  `Reports/handoff/nest-experiments-2026-10-04/README.md`. That branch is ~160
  commits behind main: **cherry-pick from it, never merge it**. Their dead-ends
  entries are PR 653 (§4.5).
- **Deletions of anything need Scott's own yes.**
- **Re-testing what was tried (Scott, 2026-10-08):** *"For things that have already been
  tried, do not reject based on a previous finding that it hurt the colony numbers. Our
  current ants/colony is very different. Only a specific explanation of why it failed
  relevant to the implemented mechanism and would still be true in our current game can be
  immediately rejected."* So a dead-ends entry whose reason is "fewer ants" or "colonies
  collapsed" is an open candidate, not a rejection; one whose reason is a mechanism (the
  rule seals the food passage, it reads a lagging average) is rejected only after checking
  that mechanism still holds on today's ant.

### 2.4 Design principles (Scott)

- **Hunger is a base need that overrides every rule.** No state (holding a pellet, a
  job, a role) may keep a starving ant from acting on hunger. The rule audit
  ([`2026-10-07/rule-audit-2026-10-06.md`](2026-10-07/rule-audit-2026-10-06.md) §5)
  turns this into "no veto without a floor": any rule that gates eating, walking to
  food or out, dropping a load, or digging out must name the hunger level at which
  it gives way.
- **Local signals.** Rules should read what an ant could sense where it stands.
  A global signal (a colony-wide count, a map of the whole nest) is allowed only as
  a **labelled stopgap**. Example: `DOOR_COLUMN` is labelled a stopgap in its doc.
- **Engine changes are fine behind their own off switch.**
- **Judge a feature by traces of the behaviour it was designed for.** Colony size is
  a floor to check, never the verdict.
- **Never reject a working fix on colony size alone.** A fix that works on its own
  metric but shrinks the colony ships, and the cost gets traced. The one hold: a fix
  that **kills colonies** stays built but off until the deaths are traced.
- **A failed test is not giving up.** Find out why.
- **Blocked upstream = INCONCLUSIVE.** If an arm failed because of a fault in
  something it depends on, it is not a failure of the arm. Re-run it when the fault
  is fixed. Example: the digging modes of 2026-10-07 (§5).
- **Dead ends are re-checkable** (2026-10-06: "I don't always trust the previous
  agents that put them there"). When a plan leans on a dead end, read the whole
  entry and its data, say how well supported it is, and re-run it when the support
  is thin. Never park a fix behind a feature that itself needs the fix.
- **Scott's ideas are hypotheses unless he says "decided".** Test them like any
  other idea; say plainly when one doesn't work.
- **Prefer general fixes.** Big redesigns need a design doc and an adversarial
  review before building.

### 2.5 The merge and default-on bar (Scott, 2026-10-07 02:56-03:05)

A switch defaults **on** and merges if it is **clearly better, or fixes a specific
traced problem**, with:

- **12 seeds to 300k** (1-4 seeds to iterate, labelled partial);
- **colony-killing stays off** until the deaths are traced;
- **a smaller colony alone is no veto**;
- **a written yes from a second reviewing session** (in the old project, the Deep
  trace lane) that the trace supports it;
- **CI green** on the head being merged.

Features default on unless there is a measured harm (owner, 2026-09-27,
`CLAUDE.md`). Bundle related parts as **one switch with named parts**
(`PIXEL_PHYSICS_X=on|off|part1,part2`), so each part can be tested alone.

### 2.7 Numbers that have misled us

Read [`../how-we-test.md`](../how-we-test.md) §4 first. Added since it was written:

- **`inside_nest` is not the nest.** It reads "ground overhead", so the mound's
  tunnels count as inside. **The mound's tunnels are not the nest.** The nest is the
  dug space below the founding ground line.
- **A deep band without the door split or the fed/staying check** (§2.6).
- **Relative gains on a near-zero base.**
- **`eats` counts bites**, not food taken. **`trip_deliveries`** is the food that
  came home; plain `deliveries` and `forage_trips` are not (most "trips" were soil
  holders looping).
- **Judging a rule on the dead only.** The dead under a rule are the ones it didn't
  save. Count the at-risk ants in both arms (findings file, "Known traps").
- **Pooled shares from one seed.** **Corpses counted as food.**
- **Sampling ants by `id % N`** picks the nest workers only (caste is by id).
- **`starvewhere.py` "encased" before PR 652**: a depth artefact.
- **`NEEDS_FIRST`'s `shut_in` can be stale**: it drove need-drops on ants that were
  no longer shut in (traced 2026-10-07).
- **The store counted crumbs too small to eat** before `NEST_STORE`'s `edible` part:
  "store can feed" read true while it held nothing an ant could eat (§4.2).
- **The chamber counter** counts maze walls and puddles as chamber boundaries.
  Confirm every "chamber" on a picture.
- **`deeptrace`'s `probe_full` read raw senses** before PR 651.
- **Broodlog `laid` rows count re-appearances**: use `stats.csv` `eggs_laid`.
- **`stats.csv` columns are cumulative**, and `hungry.csv` logs only ants under
  their 200 J grant.
- **Two arms of one seed are the same colony only until they part** (8-19k frames).

### 2.8 The standard measuring setup

The full method is [`../how-we-test.md`](../how-we-test.md). The run every recent
result used:

```bash
cargo build --release --example deeptrace
RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1 target/release/examples/deeptrace \
  scenario=nest_goal founder=evolved ants=0 hungry=1 dig=1 \
  mapevery=25000 shots=1 foodgap=90 seed=S frames=300000 out=DIR
```

- **The dry goal box** (`assets/lab_scenarios/nest_goal.ron`): no plants, one endless
  heap of player food 30 columns east of the door.
- **Heap 30 against heap 90.** `foodgap=90` moves the heap 90 columns east. Scott
  (2026-10-07 02:39): **the heap at 30 confounds results**, because the nest grows
  under it and ants start laying at the food. Run every test at **heap 90**, and at
  heap 30 as well for a default. `foodgap=` is on the stack branch and lands with
  the stack PR (655); it is not on main as of `d4eff6a5`. **A build without it
  silently ignores `foodgap=` and puts the heap at 30**, so check the branch.
- **Mutation off** (the default in every measuring example), **the evolved founder**,
  **seeds 1-4 to iterate, 12 to settle**, **300k frames** (shorter runs missed late
  collapses twice), **4 single-threaded runs at once**. A 300k run takes 5-8 minutes
  per seed at `ants=0`; full recordings are GBs, keep them out of the repo.
- **Off arm = baseline on the same build.** Check the off arm reproduces the old game
  byte for byte (`scripts/deeptrace_tools/identity.py`), then compare paired by seed.
- Before a default ships, one run with mutation **on** (2 seeds to 150k).
- Scott runs **Windows / PowerShell**. Give him commands in that form:
  `$env:PIXEL_PHYSICS_NEEDS_FIRST="on,backfill"; cargo run --release --bin lab`.

| `CARRY_HOME` (`on`) | **Food carriers get home.** A floor under the route home, and the crop is filled before leaving food. | 11-113% more trip food home on 4 of 4 seeds; carries losing the way 86-89% to 57-65% (measured; [`home-search`](2026-10-07/home-search-2026-10-05.md) §10-§13) |
| `DOOR_COLUMN` (`on`) | **No soil over the door**: pellets are never set down in the door's column, and soil that slides there is removed. **A labelled stopgap**: it is a fixed column, not something an ant senses. | Mouth open to the sky on 100% of maps against 0-1%; way in 1 step against 8-14 (measured, 150k) |
| `LAY_BAR` (`body`) | An egg is affordable from the layer's own body energy only, not food in reach. | Store fills; one hatch-wave die-off at heap 30 traced ([`laybar-body-results`](2026-10-07/laybar-body-results-2026-10-07.md)). It does **not** make food cap births (§6 step 2). |
| `NEST_STORE` (`on,pick=20,jaws,sky,meal,smell=10,edible`) | **A food store inside the nest.** Fed nest workers carry door food down and set it along the room's walls; hungry ants go in and eat there; store food is kept for the hungry; a store smell leads to it. `edible`: the store counts only food a mouth can actually eat. | §4.2 |
| `WAY_FOOT` (`on`) | **The nest's ways follow the walls, not the backs of the crowd.** A way step onto a cell held up only by ants costs 4. | Falls of hungry deep ants halved on 8 of 8 seeds; 12-seed bar below |

### Step 2. Make food cap births (traced part-way; tied to step 3)

- **Why it matters:** brood carrying and the "two crowds" room rule both failed
  with eggs rising and larvae starving (§5). Until food caps births, any rule that
  moves brood or frees egg sites makes a brood flood and a famine.
- **What the Laying trace found** (measured, the stack at heap 90, seeds 1-4,
  20-200k, a laying census of every ant every 1k frames; full write-up
  [`egg-cap/handoff.md`](2026-10-07/egg-cap/handoff.md), tables and readers beside
  it). It changes the reading of the "held for want of a free cell" counter:
  - **The body bar doesn't bite because rich ants are plentiful.** 19-27% of adults
    (117-175 ants) are above the 1,034 J bar at any moment, and every egg is
    already paid from the layer's own body. **The bar checks only the egg's price**,
    120 J. That is about a tenth of a new ant, since a pupa costs 1,140-1,290 J of
    larval food on top. **52-79% of that larval food comes from nestmates' bodies**,
    19-47% from food beside the larva, and 0-4% from crops.
  - **Eggs are held because the ants that can afford one aren't at home.** 90-96%
    of affordable ant-time has no home cell within reach. Those ants stand on the
    mound top (31-73%), in the mound's tunnels (4-50%) and on the surface (15-24%).
    **The nest's own ants are never rich**: 0-1% of ants below the ground line are
    ever above the bar.
  - **The few rich ants at the door are blocked by ants, not brood.** About 40 other
    ants and 0.1-0.9 brood cells fill the 9x9 around them. A walk allowed through
    brood would find a site only 0-3% of the time.
  - **The brood ends up in one column.** 69-90% of eggs land in the top of the door
    shaft; the larvae lie at its bottom, 10+ rows from the layers. Larvae with a
    rich adult within 2 cells fall from 11-17% to 5-6%. The larvae queue grows (121-146
    to 364-410), and larvae starved per 50k rise from 0-13 to 149-234 **on the base
    arm**.
- **What this means** (inferred, not traced): the game already had a food cap.
  `brood::nurse` makes the richest adult touching a hungry larva feed it, so "a nest
  with brood waiting finishes them before it lays more". It works only if layers
  stand beside larvae. On the stack they don't, so larvae rarely drain the ants
  that lay. **The birth cap shares its root with the number one problem: ants live
  on the mound, not in the nest.** Brood carrying probably makes this worse by
  moving larvae even farther from rich adults (untraced).
- **Next, in the note's order:**
  1. Check whether layers ever feed larvae, with no new run: join `feeds.csv` donor
     ids with broodlog `laid` parent ids. This would turn the disconnection into a
     measurement.
  2. Trace the brood-carry arm with the census (`claude/nest-race-two-crowds`
     `516a223c` plus [`egg-cap/tools/census.patch`](2026-10-07/egg-cap/tools/census.patch)).
  3. Write the food-cap proposal and have a second session review it before building.
     Draft directions, not reviewed:
     - **Feed before you lay**: an ant that could lay first gives to hungry larvae it
       can smell (`brood::larva_scent`), and lays only when it smells none. Biology:
       Orlova et al. 2020, Cassill & Tschinkel 1995. The catch is the 10-row gap
       between layers and larvae, so the cue must reach across it or the egg site
       must move to the brood.
     - **Cull young brood when food is short**: hungry larvae or adults eat eggs.
       Biology: Schmickl & Crailsheim 2001, Bizzell & Pull 2024, Pulliainen et al.
       2019.
     - **Not proposed:** an egg priced at the whole child, which would undo Scott's
       crop feeding of larvae, and a colony-wide brake (`FOOD_BRAKE` is global).
  4. Rebuild on `edible`; these runs had it off.

### Step 3. Trace why most ants live in the mound, then try local cues inward

- **The lead** (code-read, rule audit §3): no live rule pulls an idle ant inward.
  Every pull aims at the door, outside, or an anchor. **This has not been traced on
  the stack.** Start by tracing it: for idle, fed ants on the mound and in its
  tunnels, what pull (if any) acts on them each decision, and what they sense.
- **The approach: local cues, not more destination pulls.** Pulls to places
  (door, store, anchor) work but are fragile: each needs its own exceptions for
  hunger, crowding and the rest, and most recent bugs were a pull misfiring (the
  crumb trap: the store pull switched off hungry ants' walk-out). The walk has **one
  pull slot, filled first-match**, so any pull can veto hunger (rule audit §1).
- **What real ants do** (sources to check before citing in code): where an ant sits
  comes from its job and what it senses close by. Young workers stay with the brood
  and workers keep small spatial zones (Mersch et al. 2013; Sendova-Franks & Franks,
  **not checked**); depth and darkness keep ants in (Tschinkel 2004); foragers go
  out by how often returning foragers meet them (Gordon 2013); many workers are
  inactive at any moment, as a reserve (Charbonneau, Sasaki & Dornhaus 2017). See
  [`inside-01-where-real-ants-are.md`](2026-10-07/inside-01-where-real-ants-are.md)
  and [`inside-05-scott-ideas-vs-biology.md`](2026-10-07/inside-05-scott-ideas-vs-biology.md).
- **Candidate cues, one at a time, each its own switch part:** brood scent (idle
  ants drift toward brood), depth or darkness (an idle ant underground slows or
  stays), crowding (an ant moves away from a packed spot). Each scored on ants
  living deep (fed, staying, off the door column, §2.6), with the colony as a floor.
- **Make hunger safe first.** The rule audit's order (§6 there): no state may veto
  hunger, then a resting place inside. `NEEDS_FIRST` is most of the first half; the
  audit's proposed guard test (an ant at 30% of its grant in each state on the
  ladder must get a way to food within bounded ticks) is not built yet.

- **Mound diggers traced, 2026-10-08** (`Reports/mound-diggers-2026-10-08/`):
  the holes are cut by idle, fed foragers spending most of their time on the
  mound (4.1M decisions there against 2.2M in the nest, on the playtest
  stack), because the curvature dig wire reads the mound's hollows as faces.
  It is a symptom of this step's problem (ants living on the mound), and has
  its own levers too. On main 85% of all cuts are in the mound.

**What it found.**

1. **The record of plant deaths was mostly wrong, and the lab showed it** (measured,
   `15f4d790`, before the fix). FELLED booked 93-97% of all plant deaths on a bed
   with nothing to fell anything. It was the fallback for "no cells left, no cause",
   so seeds rotting on their designed clock, old age (which wrote no cause) and the
   real fellings were one pile. After the fix (measured, `12ccabe9`): seeds rotted
   8,500 / 10,617 / 9,865 / 8,969; plants felled 1,235 / 1,751 / 1,448 / 1,161;
   UNKNOWN 0 / 1 / 0 / 1 plants. With the colony on: seeds eaten 7,440 / 7,008 /
   6,757 / 7,138; UNKNOWN 3 / 1 / 3 / 3 plants and no seeds.
2. **Larvae eat stored seeds** (traced, step 1's build before the label). With the
   colony on, 4 / 23 / 179 / 12 grass seeds a run died to a removal nobody
   labelled. A backtrace taken where each seed's last cell went tied all 14 sampled
   to one write: a larva eating a stored seed in `creature::eat_toward_birth`. It is
   now booked EATEN.
3. **Grass seedlings are felled before their roots reach soil** (measured,
   `7c66a17f`). Of the grass that germinated (1,844 / 2,411 / 2,056 / 1,669), 6.8 /
   4.2 / 5.4 / 5.9% grew to seed size, and of the rest 66 / 71 / 72 / 67% were
   felled. All told 1,155 / 1,653 / 1,410 / 1,064 grass are felled a run, a median
   142 / 142 / 144 / 140 frames after germinating: one or two plant ticks. FELLED
   comes only from the support check cutting tissue loose (code-read). Registered
   as a dated note on §B2, not changed. Inferred, not traced: this is the case the
   comment at `germinate`'s structural-check site warns against. Which disturbance
   schedules a check on a seedling that young is untraced.
4. **Most roots stop at the root-share gate** (measured, `50901f84`). Of the grass
   root tips that retired (45,286 / 45,734 / 35,649 / 31,994), 94 / 99 / 94 / 95%
   retired there. A tree's root tips stop mostly for want of carbon instead: 55 /
   100 / 89 / 78% too poor (of 228 / 9 / 294 / 207). The decision log sees the same
   from the other side (measured, `d434ee28`): 93-96% of all `Grow` visits are root
   tips, and 88.6 / 95.4 / 92.1 / 90.4% of those are refused on the root share.
   Open: is the gate retiring roots for good on a passing ratio (the polarity
   review's note 1 in `../open-bugs-handoff.md`), or doing its job on plants that
   already hold as much root as their shoot can feed? The counts cannot tell.
5. **One tree's leader, visit by visit** (traced, seed 1, plant 18, `d434ee28`). It
   climbed 103 cells in 209 visits: it grew on 103, could not yet pay for a cell on
   94, and was refused near its height on 12. Its turgor headroom fell in a straight
   line from 0.90 to 0.095, and it retired at frame 18,839 after three height
   refusals in a row while holding 13x the price of a step. Its roots held at 25
   cells with no root tip at all from frame 3,000 to 6,000, and 80 of its 82
   retired root tips stopped too poor; water never ran short. One plant on one
   seed: a picture of a life, not a rate.
6. **Registered, not changed** (measured, `12ccabe9` and `7c66a17f`): roots grow
   into other plants' dormant seeds and destroy them (§W8; code-read: `growable()`
   has no owner check). Dormant leafy seeds die on the grown plant's starvation
   clock as well as their own decay clock, and 89-98% of them starve while dormant
   (§V5).
7. **The decision log is the rule's own arithmetic** (measured, `d434ee28`). `check`
   rebuilt 32,409 / 23,860 / 33,639 / 27,310 scores and replayed 6,114 / 5,053 /

### 8a.1 What I did

- **Rebuilt the bed headlessly from the actions log** (`examples/replay.rs`) and ran it

  sample and has no no-ant twin. The replay tracks the census exactly to tick 40,000
  with rain off and half-pace plants (the log records neither) and drifts after that,
  so the six seeds are Scott's bed, not his run; his playtest sits inside their range
  on every headline.
- **Ran about 60 replays in these arms** (seeds in brackets, 450k ticks unless said):
  as played (1-6) and the no-ant twin (1-6); live leaf inedible (1-6); seeds inedible
  and neither (1-2, 300k); leaf at 20 and 160 J (1-3, 300k); a tree and a conifer

### 8a.2 What I found

Six seeds, medians, unless said; per-seed values are in the report.

1. **The ants strip the stand from the bottom up, and live leaf is only 8% of what
   they eat** (measured). Plants at 250k: 11 with the colonies against 36 without.
   Leaf within 15 rows of the ground: 102 cells against 617, most of it gone within
   ~50k ticks of the colonies' arrival; the trees' high canopy is untouched. Leaf is
   25% of the bites and 8% of the energy; dead leaves are 60%. A herb with 1-10
   recorded leaf bites is ~93% bare and 47% of them are dead: **no middle**.
2. **Edibility is what matters, not the leaf's energy** (measured, seeds 1-3 for the
   dose). Leaf `food_energy` 0 / 20 / 40 (shipped) / 160 J leaves **83 / 13 / 12 / 14**
   plants at 250k. With leaf inedible (six seeds), starved larvae fall 184 -> 32 and
   adults at 250k are 80 against 12. Inedible *seeds* change nothing (2 seeds).
3. **What an edible leaf does to a colony** (measured; 300k, six seeds). Per
   adult-tick it takes in the same food (0.099 / 0.095 J) and hatches the same number
   of adults, but **lays 54% more eggs, and 30% of eggs starve as larvae (0.8% with
   leaf inedible)**; adult starvation is 2.7x; `HUNGRY_OUT` pulls 5.9x; ants carry
   home 12 seeds per million adult-ticks against 85. In the playtest itself **548 of
   1,028 eggs starved as larvae**, and 376 of 576 adult deaths were old age.
4. **Eggs laid track the share of adults above the egg bar** (measured on `ants.csv`;
   the bar, ~950 J, is *code-read*: `lay_at` 1,100 J times the founder's
   `reproduce_at`, read as linear). Share above the bar against eggs per million
   adult-ticks: inedible 4.1% / 24; as played 6.6% / 37; leaf 20 J 7.1% / 40; door
   trees 7.3% / 39; leaf 160 J 8.5% / 43. The adults' banks are more unequal whenever
   leaf is edible (Gini 0.38-0.39 against 0.33). **This links two measured
   distributions to a rule read in the code; no ant was traced from a meal to an
   egg**, so *why* an edible leaf unequalises the banks is not known.
5. **Plants interrupt nests as walls, not as an invasion** (measured). Roots and
   plant tissue closed at most 37 of the playtest's 2,141 dug cells (2.1%); a median
   19% of dig attempts at solid ground hit wood or root. **With a tree and a conifer
   planted at the two doors before the ants arrived**, one nest stayed at 31 cells
   (82% of its digs on wood) **and the colonies were still the biggest**: median peak
   208 adults against 124, 21% of forage trips bringing food home against 13% (4
   seeds, 8 colonies; *inferred*: a food source the ants cannot strip, at the door).
6. **A fixed plant defence did not stand in for inedibility** (measured, 11 runs,
   220k): 0.2-0.9 cut the litter standing when the ants arrived by 40-77%
   (147 -> 90 ... 34 cells), the colonies' intake fell 2,103 -> 22 kJ, and the herbs
   were still not saved. With mutation on, plant defence rose 0.025 -> 0.035 in 300k
   ticks.
7. **The bed's rain changed how far the stand collapsed, not whether the low foliage
   was stripped** (measured, two seeds). Under the shipped light rain the no-ant stand
   was 4x bigger and the colonies cut it 18-57% instead of 62-80%, while the reachable
   foliage was still stripped 77%. With rain off and no ants the stand died out by
   itself (5-16 plants at 1.5M ticks). **Scott's playtest had its rain off and nothing
   in the chronicle says so**: the actions log omits `CycleRain`, `CyclePlantPace`,
   `ToggleWindfallRot` and the lamps.

**How it sits beside the other sections.** The nest-life session's §6 section reports
colonies "not overbreeding" in the food-only goal box. In the plant bed, with
`LAY_BRAKE=on` already set, the surplus eggs appeared only when leaf was edible (item
3), so the two results are about different beds. §10 (plant tracing) found larvae
eating stored seeds and booked seeds eaten in the thousands a run; the `EATEN` trap in
8a.4 is the same thing seen from the census. Ants that can eat leaf carried far fewer seeds home (item 3),
which touches the seed-cargo and garden lane notes (`evolution-lab-seed-cargo.md`,
`evolution-lab-garden-*.md`): a seed rides home only if the ant bit it and it
survived.

### 8a.4 What cost time

- **"Afield" is not "above ground".** 66-83% of adults are above ground at any
  moment; the census's `ants_afield` is beyond 40 columns of every nest. I first read
  "94% of starvers died above ground" as foraging deaths, which was wrong.
- **`brood_larvae_hungry` equals `brood_larvae`** in every row (a fed larva pupates
  at once); `larvae_starved / eggs_laid` is the quantity that varies.
- **The `*_j` columns read 0 with no ants** and move with the gut (litter 388.8 J a
  cell at gut -0.8, 120 at gut 0), so I counted cells.
- **`EATEN` in the death histogram is mostly dormant seeds** (3,200 in a run where
  ants eat seeds instead of leaf), not plants killed.
- **Crumbs dilute a J-weighted bite distance**: half-eaten leftovers are 46-68% of the
  bitten joules and sit inside the nest, so I left them out.
- **`docscheck` exits 1 locally under Python 3.13** (a DeprecationWarning from
  `deadendindex.py` is counted as a failure; with `PYTHONWARNINGS=ignore` it is clean,
  and CI's passes). The plant-tracing session saw the same.

## 8b. Brood and the dig: the 2026-10-09 brood write-up

*Added 2026-10-10 by the session that swept every branch for handoffs that never
reached this page. The work was done on 2026-10-09 by a session on
`claude/dazzling-bohr-9pa49e` (`c40c65f2`), which wrote a full report and **no
section here and no PR**. The report is now on main:
[`../brood-and-the-dig-2026-10-09.md`](../brood-and-the-dig-2026-10-09.md). **The
switch, the five counters, their eight guards, `examples/deeptrace.rs`'s new columns
and `scripts/deeptrace_pairs.py` are still only on that branch**; nothing in this
section is on main as code. Measured on the nest-race stack, heap 90, evolved founder,
seeds 1-4 paired, 300k frames.*

Scott's question (2026-10-09): *"see if brood or larvae on the ground will affect the
dig behaviour. Can an ant dig under a larva?"*, and then whether the way the dig reads
a larva could be why **the lab's nest is always one huge room**.

What it found (marks as in the report):

- **An ant can dig under a larva, and does** (traced, guards on a real frame loop).
  The jaw never takes brood, but a nest worker's face turn cuts the floor cell under a
  larva lying in its way; the larva drops into the pit and lives. Rare: 0.15-0.19% of
  cuts (measured).
- **The dig reads a larva as ground in two places** (code-read, then run): the
  curvature sense (`surface_curvature`) and the heap cue's sky test
  (`open_to_the_sky`). The sky test is inert (one dig roll in 6,000). The curvature
  one matters: a larva is in reach on 56-61% of dig rolls and decides "enclosed" on
  7-9.5%.
- **`PIXEL_PHYSICS_BROOD_BLIND=curv,sky|on`** (off; on the branch only) hides brood
  from both. On: dig-down turns 7.3-8.8% -> 3.8% of won rolls on every seed, fewer
  cuts on 4 of 4, larvae starved lower on 4 of 4 (1,097/1,011/880/1,095 ->
  391/884/839/797; **unexplained**).
- **Brood is not why the nest is one room** (measured, 4 seeds a side). The nest
  stays one room with brood hidden (8 of 8 runs at 100k and 200k, 7 of 8 at 300k, the
  exception a run where brood was *not* hidden), as it did under the earlier
  `BROOD=off` and `BROOD_DIG` controls. Digging is not drawn to brood (0.31x what the
  walls offer). The report says this idea "should not be repeated".
- **What the diggers do instead** (measured, a description not a cause): they stay
  put (77% of next cuts within 2 cells) but work a 9x9 patch, advancing a third of a
  cell a cut, all round the rim, trimming bumps. A tunnel would advance a cell a cut.
  **Persistence without advance.**

Not established: four seeds a side only (seeds 5-12 were started and their result is
not recorded anywhere); the `curv` and `sky` halves were not run apart; depth at 300k
is clipped by bedrock; the fewer starved larvae are untraced.

Its suggested next measurement (not done): trace one long digging bout cut by cut
(heading, the cell faced, the cell cut, and why the next cut was not further along),
using `digrows.csv.gz` and `deeptrace_dig.py face`, with the `funnel` skill. If the
heading does not persist between cuts, the cut chooser is what turns a line into a
patch.
