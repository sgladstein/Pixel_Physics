Analysis only · no new runs · 52 batches, 21–24 Oct 2026

Are we spending the VM well?

Every in-run and colony-selection batch in the cloud bucket, re-read from its own tables: 209,000 colony-rounds and 83,000 garden runs, about 1,100 CPU-hours for the colony-selection batches alone. The question is how much of that bought a decision.

Short answers

  • Control arms
    The comparison is needed. Paying for it fresh usually was not. Judging the new idea alone, against where it started, matches the real call on only 9 of 18 decision measures; 3 come out backwards, because selection alone improves a colony a lot. But of the 31 fresh same-batch controls, 6 were byte-identical reruns of an earlier arm, 13 confirmed effects large enough that an earlier run already settled them, 7 found no effect, and 5 changed or protected the call.
  • Reruns
    The simulator is deterministic, so the same build, founder, settings and seeds give the same numbers. 7% of all colony-rounds were exact repeats of something already in the bucket.
  • Gardens
    The common garden runs after every second round and costs 30% of colony-selection CPU. Calls were read from one or two of its 24 gardens.
  • Colonies
    64 is right when the effect is medium-sized. 32 colonies keep 99% of large-effect calls and 85% of medium ones, and no subsample at any size ever reversed a call. Pairing arms by seed buys nothing after 48 rounds: the same colony in two arms is uncorrelated (r ≈ 0).
  • Rounds
    Rule changes (body rules, inheritance) show in the first garden. Learned behaviour (light dig head) settles by round 23–31. Erosion questions, like the nest peaking and then shrinking, need the full 48.

Net

Two mechanical changes, gardens every 8 rounds and no exact reruns, would have cut about 28% of CPU with no change to any decision on this page: about 1.4 batches for the price of one. A screening stage and shared controls go further, at the cost of slower answers on small effects.

Two separate questions. First, does an idea need a comparison at all, or can we run it alone and see whether the colony got better? Second, when we did run a control in the same batch, did that arm tell us anything an earlier run had not?

How many colonies?

For every pair of arms in the same colony-selection batch and five measures (nest, crater, starved, home food, alive), I took the call at 64 colonies and redrew it 300 times from random subsets of fewer colonies. 176 pair-measures had a call at 64.

Share of 64-colony calls still made with fewer colonies

  • Large effect (more than 1 spread)
  • Medium (0.5 to 1 spread)
  • Small (under 0.5 spread)

Effect size is the mean colony-by-colony difference divided by its spread. Dashed line: 32 colonies. No subset at any size gave the opposite call; fewer colonies only turn calls into "can't tell".

The decisions that moved the line were mostly large: the roof and room rules needed about 6–10 colonies, the light dig head about 10, the hcs founder 24. The ones that needed 40–56 colonies (inheritance retry, the distance ramp's nest cost) were medium effects. Spread between colonies is wide: the per-arm spread is about 0.6× the median for nest size, 1× for crater, 0.4× for home food, and nearly 2× for starved, so a single colony means little.

One caveat the data cannot settle: in colony selection the colonies are also the population being selected (the bottom half is refounded from the top half). Fewer colonies would change the training, not only the measurement. That needs one paired test before a smaller standard is adopted.

The older in-run batches (per-seed jobs, 6 rounds) show the same pattern with smaller effects: of 92 arm pairs, 37 had a food call at 64 seeds, and the middle effect size was 0.23 of the spread. There seed pairing did help a little (r ≈ 0.3 between arms on the same seed); in 48-round colony selection it does not.

How many rounds?

Does an early garden already show the round-47 call?

  • Same call as at round 47
  • Opposite call

131 pair-measures from 48-round batches that had a call at round 47. The rest of each column is "no call yet". Round 47 is itself one noisy sample, so this understates early agreement a little.

By round 15 about 6 in 10 calls already match and 1 in 12 points the other way; past round 29 almost nothing reverses. For the decisions that mattered the picture is sharper: rule changes such as the roof rule and the failed inheritance retry were clear from the first garden, after two rounds. Learned changes took longer, and questions about erosion (nest peaks at round 15–21 and then shrinks, as in colsel4 and the roof arm) only show in long runs.

What to change

  1. Run the garden every 8 rounds, not every 2.≈ 22% CPU

    Rounds 7, 15, 23, 31, 39, 47 still show the trajectory and the peak-then-erode shape. CG_EVERY=8 already exists in colsel.py; only the default changes.

  2. Treat a control as a cache entry, never a rerun.≈ 6–8% CPU

    Key it by the build's simulation code, founder pool, settings and seeds. If an arm with the same key is in the bucket, point the comparison at it. Also resume shared prefixes instead of replaying them: colsel4 replayed colsel3's 16 rounds, colsel10 replayed 32 rounds of colsel9b, and the weaned control replayed 9 rounds of colsel18.

  3. One control per ratchet turn, shared by every idea in that turn.½ to ¼ of control cost

    Under the ratchet the founder changes each time, so a cached control lasts one turn. Testing three or four ideas against one control (as colsel15 and colsel20 did) spreads it. Ratchet when a batch clearly beats the line rather than after every batch, so the control lives longer.

  4. Screen first, then confirm.up to 3–5× per idea

    A 32-colony, 16-round screen with gardens at 7 and 15 costs about a sixth of a full arm. It catches large effects (all the rule changes here) and almost never points the wrong way. Only ideas that pass, plus anything aimed at late-game erosion, get the full 64 × 48.

  5. Name the deciding measure before the batch.fewer false wins

    Most batches read five measures. With five chances at p < 0.05, an idea that does nothing shows a "significant" change on at least one about one time in five.

Other things worth checking

  • Gardens drop extinct colonies. The moving-food arms ended with 47–56 colonies in the garden while others had 64, so their medians describe survivors only. Counting an extinct colony as zero alive and zero nest makes arms comparable.
  • Lab survival is a separate test. farfood1's control looked fine in the bed and still starved in the owner's box; the bed garden alone would not have caught it. A small lab-box garden on the finalist (16 colonies) is cheap insurance.
  • Small effects are being decided anyway. cut_join + crater and rules-off-from-round-0 were adopted on differences the data barely separate. That is a fair call when the change is cheap to undo; it is worth saying so on the decision card.
  • The +lab case and spider cases add per-round cost that this audit could not separate from training time; worth one timing line in colsel's log.
