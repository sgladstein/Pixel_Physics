Lone scout seat · rough look · seed 1, the same colony in both clips

You are one worker. You lead sisters to a far heap, one at a time, by touch.

The colony is a trained one, hungry, with a little food near the nest and a big heap far to the east. You pick a sister, tap her, and walk. She follows with her head on your back, stops now and then to look around, and you have to stop with her or she loses you. When she reaches the heap she has been shown the way.

The left clip is that. The right clip is the design you turned down, given equal terms: you call, and every willing sister nearby falls in behind you and never stops or strays. Same box, same colony, same player script, 6,000 frames each (about 100 seconds at 1×), one shot every 40 frames.

What the marks mean
You (white, with a ring).

A sister who has been shown the heap (brighter means a better lesson).
Dotted line: who taught whom. A red cross over a sister means she refused.

What this does not show yet

  • A taught sister does not go back on her own yet. Today's trained ants ignore their memory of where food was (that weight is zeroed in the pool), so being shown the heap changes nothing after the first trip. The keeper seat is building the piece that makes a trained ant act on a remembered place; until it lands, the "trips home afterwards" row is luck, and B wins it by leading more ants.
  • One seed. The colony booms and busts on its own, so 94 → 86 against 94 → 107 is not a result. Paired runs over four seeds come next.
  • The cascade (sisters you taught teaching others) has not fired in any run yet.
  • This is the binary's own seat, played by a script that walks, taps and waits. Fixed today on the way: the pupil touching your tail used to count as three cells away and froze you both; pupils never "arrived" once the heap was eaten down; and the heap's next drop buried the player when she stood on it.
