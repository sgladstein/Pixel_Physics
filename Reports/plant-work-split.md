## 1. The finding that prompted the form session

The owner's reading of a conifer sheet against a tree sheet, both at the
same crop and the same noon frame: **"they still look pretty similar —
look at a real conifer versus an oak."** That is correct, and this
session's earlier claim that they "read as two different species" was
grading on a curve. It is withdrawn.

**What the real contrast is.** A spruce has one central leader unbroken
from base to tip, branches in whorls coming off near-horizontal, each tier
longer than the one above — the outline is a **cone**, and the needles are
dense enough that a branch reads as a solid plume rather than a stick with
things on it. An oak stops having a trunk partway up and divides into
several codominant limbs that spread and gnarl — the outline is a **broad
dome**, foliage a shell over a shaded open interior. Cone against dome:
distinguishable in silhouette, at night, at a hundred metres.

**What we have.** Both species are an irregular tangle of brown sticks
with green speckle. The measured differences are hue, ~20% height, and a
slightly different crown blob:

| | shrub | tree | conifer |
|---|---|---|---|
| organism cells | 15,887 | 27,048 | 41,399 |
| height, rows | 80 | 158 | 189 |
| thickest crown run | 35 | 49 | 63 |
| forks (sympody) | 39-89 | 0 | 0 |
| **leaf fraction** | **26%** | **31%** | **26%** |

(8 plants / 30,000 frames / `worldseed=1`. Sheets:
`target/filmstrips/noon-conifer.png` and `noon-tree-samecrop.png`,
identical crop, both at noon — see §2c on why noon matters.)

### Three candidate causes, none of which is "tune the existing knobs"

**a. Foliage is a garnish, not a mass.** Leaf fraction is 26-31% and it
renders as dots strung along a branch. On a real conifer the needles *are*
the silhouette and the wood is invisible. Here the wood skeleton sets the
outline in every species, and that skeleton is generated by the same rules
for all three — so they converge. This is the direct continuation of the
sympody-phase root cause `CLAUDE.md` records (then ~5% leaf, now ~28%:
real progress, same conclusion).

**b. There is no age channel, so there can be no cone.** A conifer's taper
comes from laterals being *longer the older they are* — the bottom tier
has had thirty years to extend, the top one a season. Every branch
parameter here keys on `ByOrder`, and `tree.ron`'s own comment already
names the gap: *"order is position in the plant and not age."* Without
age, laterals cannot be length-graded, so no taper and no cone. The same
gap blocks the oak, whose codominant limb division is an age/size-
triggered event rather than a positional one.

**c. A species cannot declare what it is made of.** Three hardcoded
lookups, not species data:

```
src/sim/plant.rs germinate()  -> id_of("wood")      the shoot
src/sim/plant.rs germinate()  -> id_of("rootwood")  the companion root
src/sim/plant.rs Grow arm     -> id_of("leaf")      leaf placement
```

Growth then propagates the parent's material — deliberately; the comment
says *"every cell `Grow` creates copies its parent's material ... with no
cell-type-to-material table anywhere."* Good decision, but it means the
material is seeded once, identically, for every species. `moss.ron`
escapes only by being a `Divide` species on a separate planting path with
no roots, leaves or thickening. The owner's framing: *other plants
shouldn't have wood* — today they cannot not have it.

**Recommended framing for the form session:** the question is not "what is
a plant made of" but **"what sets a plant's silhouette, and which of those
channels does the engine not have?"** Materials is one answer, foliage
mass is a second, an age channel is a third — and (b) is the one that
unlocks the conifer/oak contrast specifically. Audit all three before
committing to a direction.

## 2. Measured this session; do not re-derive

**a. Colour is already a readout, and it works.** Foliage band = leaf
economy allele, bark band = wood density allele; both heritable, both
mutable, both legible at play zoom. Pinned stands come out uniform in one
band (wood band 0: 22,866 cells / band 1: 15,347) against a mixed stand's
9,666 / 6,130. Do not propose a colour mechanism without reading
`plant-genome-design.md` §6 and §8b first — within-band tone is
*reserved* for live state (drought pallor, age), which has not landed.

**b. The leaf-economy locus has a real crossover.** Wet, the expensive
leaf wins (+21% mass, +32% seed); dry, the cheap one wins and holds **43%
more foliage**. The acquisitive stand on thin soil runs a 14x water
deficit and drinks its own bed to the wilting point. Foliage *cost* is
already a live trade, so any foliage-mass proposal has to compose with it.

**c. Sheets must be captured at noon.** The day period is 3,600 frames.
`scripts/megastudy.sh`'s own frames (15,000 / 30,000 / 45,000) land at
half-light, night and deepest midnight — two of three tiles unusable for
any colour or silhouette judgement. Use multiples of 3,600.

**d. `soil=N` exists now** (`PlantScene::soil_depth`, landed `04a03ff`),
so deep-against-thin is one flag rather than a recompile.

**e. Species `.ron` is `include_str!`.** Rebuild release examples between
every sweep point. Identical output across settings means the knob was
never connected.

## 3. What the genome session still owns

Branch `plant-genome`, merged into `plant-substrate-v2`. Suite green (485)
and clippy clean.

**Remaining §4 verification rows (5 of 9):** slot 5 root-tropism depth
histogram; slot 8 penetration (needs a sand-bank scene); slot 6 stand
spread; the `seed_cost` endowment curve; and the unexplained wet-stomatal
anomaly (§8e).

**Blocked on an owner call: the root economy.** Genotype slot 1 (root
branch chance) is wired correctly and never fires — a root tip must hold
two growth steps' carbon in one tick and holds about a fifth of one; 351
chances, 2 affordable, 0 fired. Branching *would* matter (root cells with
3+ root neighbours 10% to 55%: sparse diving strands become a fibrous
mat), so the finding is **repair, not retirement**. The question is
whether a root tip finishing on 0.053 carbon is the intended economy.
**This also gates the megastudy** — if root allocation changes, root mass
moves several-fold and every population number re-baselines.
