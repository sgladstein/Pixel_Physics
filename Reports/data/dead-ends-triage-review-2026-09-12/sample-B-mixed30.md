## E01  (section: destruction, register line 556)

- **src/sim/rigid.rs struct Hinge and fn swing_on_hinge; making a severed crown sweep by imposing a shared rigid-body velocity on its fragments, rejected in play 2026-08-29** - Built to answer the owner's own words -- *"it should hinge at the trunk hit the ground and the bottom branches break off from the impact... not just unzip and fall to the ground"* -- and it does exactly that on a contact sheet: every fragment off one severance shares a `Hinge {pivot, alpha, omega}`, its velocity is `omega x r` about the stump while it holds one, the base lands first, and `landed` releases it on impact. It measured well (`log` 587 -> 772 cells with the leaf fix, more wood surviving than either arm before it). **It was rejected on sight, twice, and the reason is the mechanism rather than the tuning**: *"A still looks like everything breaking but because of an invisible force pushing things around, not because it fell and hit the ground"*, then *"you are trying to force a full motion and break up... It seems like you are trying to create an animation and not a physics system that results in realistic damage."* `Hinge` **imposes** a velocity field computed at the moment of the cut; nothing in the world causes it, so it produces the right silhouette by construction and no correct cause. The model wanted instead is causal: a severed trunk **detaches** rather than fragmenting at the cut, goes over because it is unstable, and the *impact* generates the stress that breaks limbs -- `Reports/tree-mechanics-plan-2026-08-29.md` sections 2-4 in their own order. **The condition:** this is rejected as a *substitute* for the stress model, not as arithmetic -- `angular_acceleration` and `cut_face` are sound and would be reusable if a real instability ever needs a pivot and a rate. Do not reinstate the imposed velocity; anything that draws the motion rather than deriving it will read the same way. Full account, with the numbers and both verdicts: `Reports/tree-fall-2026-08-29.md` section 9d.

## E08  (section: plants, register line 909)

- **`src/sim/plant.rs` fn allocate_to_frontier, the donor sort; measured 2026-08-24** - **Caching the sort key to cut the donor re-sort's world lookups.** The per-frontier-cell `order.sort_unstable_by(|a, b| world.carbon_at(b.0, b.1).total_cmp(&world.carbon_at(a.0, a.1)))` calls `carbon_at` twice per *comparison*, so the sort alone costs `O(donors log donors)` world lookups and the loop `O(frontier x donors log donors)` — measured as the one superlinear pass in `step_organisms` (0.35 us/cell at 202 cells ticked rising to 1.94 us/cell at 969, while every sibling pass stays flat). Reading each donor's carbon once into the element and sorting on the cached `f32` looks exactly equivalent and **is not**: it changes the element type from `(i32, i32)` to `(f32, (i32, i32))`, and Rust's `sort_unstable_by` (ipnsort) specialises its small-sort strategy on element type and size, so the two take different code paths and order **equal** elements differently. Donor carbon is equal constantly — mature cells sit pinned at `RESOURCE_SCALE`, which the function's own comment describes as "the trunk sits at the cap" — so the tie order decides which donor is drained, and the stand diverges: measured on `plant_probe frames=6000 trees=4`, tree heights 101 -> 103, stem thickness 9 -> 6, root depth histogram [49, 43, 7] -> [47, 38, 13], cells shed to starvation 27 -> 56. Verified as a real divergence and not harness noise by running the baseline binary twice, which was byte-identical.
  *Re-test when:* The bit-identity half is permanent while the sort's tie order is left to the standard library — **the lookup win and bit-identity are mutually exclusive here**, because the key can only be cached by putting it in the element. It reopens the moment an explicit positional tie-break is added to the comparator (`.then(a.cmp(b))`), which makes the order a function of the comparator alone and therefore independent of both the element type and the library's implementation. That is a behaviour change in its own right and is an owner decision, not a free one — but note the standing risk it would remove: **as written, a Rust upgrade that retunes ipnsort can silently change how every plant in the world grows**, and nothing in the suite would catch it.

## E13  (section: destruction, register line 558)

- **src/sim/rigid.rs fn ChunkBody::centre_of; deriving the turn pivot from the body's current offsets on every turn, 2026-08-29** - The tidy version of "turn about the centre", and it walks. ## E17  (section: plants, register line 601)

- **`Behavior::Reproduce::seed_launch` on `tree`, as the fix for a tree whose seed never takes; measured 2026-09-05** - **A tree is budget-limited, not distance-limited, and throwing its seed costs it seeds.** The reasoning was sound and the measurement kills it: a tree draws the ground under itself below its own seedlings' `soil_water_threshold`, so its seed rains onto ground the parent has made unsuitable, and the ecological answer to that is to get the seed clear of the parent rather than lower the parent's standards. Paired across four world seeds, one founder, 60,000 frames, `launch=0` against `launch=12` -- borne / germinated / established / columns: **211/37/4/119 -> 150/13/2/137; 9/6/2/13 -> 5/4/1/9; 17/8/0/1 -> 9/4/0/2; 199/26/4/162 -> 112/17/2/117**. Seeds **borne** fall on 4 of 4, germinations fall on 4 of 4, established falls or ties on 4 of 4; only columns are mixed (up 2, down 2). The mechanism is `launch_price` -- `1.0 + LAUNCH_PRICE_PER_ROOT_CELL * sqrt(reach)`, so reach 12 is **1.87x per seed** -- against a species that sets 211 seeds in 60,000 frames from a 4,000-cell body. Halving an output that small costs more than the distance buys. **The contrast is the finding**: the same knob on `herb` measured +38% distant plants and 176 columns against 141, because a herb has seed to spare. A dispersal lever priced per seed is a good trade only for a species that is not already seed-limited, and which species those are is a measurement rather than an intuition.
  *Re-test when:* A mature tree's reproductive budget stops being a fraction of a surplus that goes to zero at its own ceiling -- `allocate_to_frontier` sets `reproductive_share = surplus * reproductive_allocation` and `surplus = income - maintenance`, which is ~0 exactly when the tree is biggest. A tree with real seed output to spend might well afford the throw, and the arm is one `launch=` away.
