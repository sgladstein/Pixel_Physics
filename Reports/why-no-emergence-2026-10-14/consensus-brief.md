I am checking six literature claims for a report about why a 2D cellular-automaton simulation of an ant colony
fails to produce an emergent, realistic nest. Please check each claim against the peer-reviewed literature, say
whether it is supported, contradicted or unaddressed, and cite the papers (with verbatim quotes where possible).

**Claim 1 (the central one):** No published model (agent-based, cellular automaton, continuum or otherwise)
produces functionally differentiated nest chambers — e.g. a brood chamber, a food/seed storage chamber and
resting areas — emerging from local individual rules, in a colony that must also forage, feed itself and
reproduce (i.e. with colony demography/energy dynamics coupled to construction). Search hard for any
counter-example: integrated whole-colony models of ants or termites that include nest construction together with
brood care, food storage and foraging; models where brood or food placement and excavation co-determine
architecture; models of nest architecture coupled to colony growth (e.g. Buhl et al. 2004/2005, Rasse &
Deneubourg 2001, the 2024/2025 eLife paper on *Camponotus fellah* colony demographics and nest construction,
Tschinkel's work), and termite mound models coupling agents to environmental fields (e.g. Ocko, Mahadevan and
colleagues).

**Claim 2:** Published construction models each reproduce essentially one architectural feature in a controlled
setting, mostly with a fixed number of agents and no energy economy. Verify: Bonabeau et al. 1998 (pillars,
walls and a royal chamber from a cement pheromone plus a queen pheromone template), Khuong et al. 2016 PNAS
(pillars and roofs from a building pheromone whose lifetime is highly influential, plus a body-size template),
Toffin et al. 2009 PNAS (round-to-branched shape transition with digger density), Buhl et al. 2004 (self-organised
digging, nest volume vs colony size), Pitonakova & Bullock 2013, Ladley & Bullock 2005. Is the characterisation
fair?

**Claim 3:** The local cue that sets vertical stratification of chambers in subterranean ant nests is unknown.
Tschinkel proposed the soil CO2 gradient as a template; Tschinkel 2013 (PLOS ONE, *Pogonomyrmex badius*) vented
the gradient and also reversed it, and nest architecture was unchanged. Confirm both the vented and the
reversed-gradient results and whether any later work proposed a replacement cue.

**Claim 4:** Tunnel stability in granular soil depends on grain size, moisture/cohesion (capillary forces) and
arching; dry cohesionless sand cannot hold tunnels; laboratory ant excavation uses moist or cohesive substrate
(e.g. Espinoza & Santamarina 2010 *Granular Matter*; Buarque de Macedo et al. 2021 PNAS; Aguilar et al. 2018
*Science* used wetted glass beads; Gravish et al.).

**Claim 5:** In narrow tunnels ant traffic clogs, and ants mitigate it by unequal workload/idleness and by
retreating (Aguilar et al. 2018 *Science*, fire ants). Are there other studies on congestion at nest entrances or
in narrow tunnels, and on where idle workers rest relative to traffic routes?

**Claim 6:** In real ant nests brood is kept in particular chambers away from the entrance and moved according to
temperature/humidity gradients, and foragers occupy upper chambers near the entrance while nurses stay deeper with
the brood (e.g. Tschinkel's harvester-ant work; Mersch et al. 2013 on spatial fidelity in *Camponotus*). Confirm
with citations.

**Output:** for each claim, a verdict (supported / partly supported / contradicted / not addressed), the key
papers with year, journal and a short quote, and especially any counter-example to Claim 1.
